What does it cost to complete a set?

Every set we cover, costed three ways, from the commons run to the full master set. These are live prices, not a number somebody typed into an article two years ago, so they move every night with the market.

The base set is the cheap part

Every card numbered up to the printed total, the ones that read 131/131 and below, is usually a small bill: $14 to $187 across these 23 sets. The money is all above that line, in the secret rares. Ascended Heroes is $78 as a base set and $6,251 as a master set. If you have been putting off completing a set because you assumed it ran to thousands, it is worth checking which half you actually want.

Three ways to finish

What counts as complete?

The word means three different things depending who is saying it, and the gap between them is most of the cost. Pick the one you actually want before you start buying.

The commons run

$14.39 average per set

Every Common and Uncommon in the numbered set. This is the part that falls out of packs by the hundred, so it is nearly free to buy and nearly worthless to sell. A good first target: it is most of the card count and almost none of the money, and it teaches you how a checklist works.

The base set

$45.45 average per set

Every card numbered up to the printed total. This is what most people mean by completing a set, and it is the tier this page sorts by. It includes the rares and the double rares but stops before the secret rares, which is exactly where the price cliff is.

The master set

$1,769 average per set

Everything, secret rares included. Several times the base set in every case, and in some sets a single card is more than a quarter of the bill on its own. Worth knowing that most master-set collectors also chase every reverse holo, which is a further tier again and not costed here.

The one card problem

When the set is one card

In 7 of these 23 sets, a single card is at least a quarter of the entire master-set bill. Completing those sets is not really a grind, it is one purchase with a long tail attached, and it is worth seeing that number before you decide the set is out of reach.

  • Phantasmal Flames. Mega Charizard X ex is $716 of a $1,256 master set, which is 57% of it. The other 127 cards come to $540 between them.
  • Paldean Fates. Mew ex is $1,014 of a $2,773 master set, which is 37% of it. The other 244 cards come to $1,759 between them.
  • Chaos Rising. Mega Greninja ex is $213 of a $708 master set, which is 30% of it. The other 121 cards come to $496 between them.
  • Prismatic Evolutions. Umbreon ex is $1,471 of a $5,038 master set, which is 29% of it. The other 179 cards come to $3,567 between them.
  • Twilight Masquerade. Greninja ex is $376 of a $1,364 master set, which is 28% of it. The other 224 cards come to $989 between them.
  • Pitch Black. Mega Darkrai ex is $233 of a $879 master set, which is 27% of it. The other 119 cards come to $646 between them.
  • Destined Rivals. Team Rocket's Mewtwo ex is $508 of a $1,924 master set, which is 26% of it. The other 243 cards come to $1,416 between them.

Two different prices

The cards are nearly free. The postage is not.

Every figure above is market price, meaning what copies have actually been selling for. There is a second number worth knowing: the cheapest copy listed right now. On the cards that make up a base set those two are miles apart, and on the cards that make up a master set they are almost the same.

The reason is that a three cent common's cheapest copy is a penny, while a $1,119 chase card's cheapest copy is close to what it sells for. So the money in a base set is not really in the cards, it is in getting 86 of them into one envelope. Mega Evolution is the extreme case: $25 at market against $3 in cheapest listings, a 7.6x gap.

We do not publish the cheapest-listing total as a set price, because it is not one. Those are single listings from 86 different sellers in unstated condition, so nobody can actually buy a set at that number. It is here to show you where the real cost sits, which is postage and patience rather than the cards.

How this was worked out

  • Cheapest printing, every time. A card that exists as a normal and a reverse holo is counted at whichever is cheaper, because that is what somebody finishing a set would buy. It makes these totals lower than the price the same card shows on our card pages, which quote a specific printing.
  • Before shipping, and on a base set that is the whole bill. Singles come from many sellers, so a real completion pays postage over and over. Add up the cheapest listing for every card in a base set and it comes to $19 on average, against $45 at market. The cards are close to free. What you are actually buying is 86 separate envelopes, which is why people do this in bulk lots from one seller and not one card at a time.
  • Market price, not the price you will pay, and it cuts both ways. Market is what copies have been selling for. Some cards can be had below it right now from somebody clearing stock, and some cannot be had at all, because the cheap commons in any set are often listed by nobody. So this is a middle estimate rather than a floor or a ceiling: expect to beat it on the cards everyone has and to pay over it on the last few.
  • Reverse holos are not costed. A full reverse-holo run is a separate tier again, and it is a big one. The master-set figures here are one copy of each card number, not one of each printing.
  • No pull rates, no pack maths. We have not costed "how many packs to complete this", because it would need pull rates we do not have. Anyone quoting you that number is estimating.

Prices are TCGplayer market via TCGdex, read August 12, 2026, and refresh on their own overnight. Card counts are the published checklists. Totals are computed from those two things and nothing else: no estimates, no rounding up to a nicer number. No affiliate links, and nothing here is a recommendation to buy anything.
