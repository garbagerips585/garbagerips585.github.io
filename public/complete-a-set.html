The base set is the cheap part

Every card numbered up to the printed total, the ones that read 131/131 and below, is usually a small bill: $20 to $1,035 across these 29 sets. The money is all above that line, in the secret rares. Ascended Heroes is $126 as a base set and $5,564 as a master set. If you have been putting off completing a set because you assumed it ran to thousands, it is worth checking which half you actually want.

Three ways to finish

What counts as complete?

The word means three different things depending who is saying it, and the gap between them is most of the cost. Pick the one you actually want before you start buying.

The commons run

$24.25 average per set

Every Common and Uncommon in the numbered set. This is the bulk, so it is nearly free to buy and nearly worthless to sell. A good first target: it is most of the card count and almost none of the money, and it teaches you how a checklist works.

The base set

$136 average per set

Every card numbered up to the printed total. This is what most people mean by completing a set, and it is the tier this page sorts by. It includes the rares and the double rares but stops before the secret rares, which is exactly where the price cliff is.

The master set

$1,437 average per set

Everything, secret rares included. Several times the base set in every case, and in some sets a single card is more than a quarter of the bill on its own. Worth knowing that most master-set collectors also chase every reverse holo, which is a further tier again and not costed here.

The one card problem

When the set is one card

In 8 of these 29 sets, a single card is at least a quarter of the entire master-set bill. Completing those sets is not really a grind, it is one purchase with a long tail attached, and it is worth seeing that number before you decide the set is out of reach.

  • Phantasmal Flames. Mega Charizard X ex is $731 of a $1,267 master set, which is 58% of it. The other 129 cards come to $535 between them.
  • Celebrations. Mew is $57 of a $108 master set, which is 53% of it. The other 24 cards come to $51 between them.
  • Silver Tempest. Lugia V is $491 of a $953 master set, which is 52% of it. The other 214 cards come to $462 between them.
  • Paldean Fates. Mew ex is $872 of a $2,525 master set, which is 35% of it. The other 244 cards come to $1,653 between them.
  • Prismatic Evolutions. Umbreon ex is $1,313 of a $4,557 master set, which is 29% of it. The other 179 cards come to $3,244 between them.
  • Chaos Rising. Mega Greninja ex is $193 of a $678 master set, which is 28% of it. The other 121 cards come to $485 between them.
  • Destined Rivals. Team Rocket's Mewtwo ex is $498 of a $1,811 master set, which is 27% of it. The other 243 cards come to $1,314 between them.
  • Pitch Black. Mega Darkrai ex is $225 of a $851 master set, which is 26% of it. The other 119 cards come to $626 between them.

Card scans are TCGdex's, and each one is the card named beside it. TCGdex publishes one scan per card number, so a card counted here at its reverse holo price is still shown in the artwork both printings share.

Two different prices

The cards are nearly free. The postage is not.

Every figure above is market price, meaning what copies have actually been selling for. There is a second number worth knowing: the cheapest copy listed right now. On the cards that make up a base set those two are miles apart, and on the cards that make up a master set they are almost the same.

The reason is that a three cent common's cheapest copy is a penny, while a $977 chase card's cheapest copy is close to what it sells for. So the money in a base set is not really in the cards, it is in getting 138 of them into one envelope. Pitch Black is the extreme case: $35 at market against $3 in cheapest listings, a 12.6x gap.

We do not publish the cheapest-listing total as a set price, because it is not one. Those are single listings from 138 different sellers in unstated condition, so nobody can actually buy a set at that number. It is here to show you where the real cost sits, which is postage and patience rather than the cards.

How this was worked out

  • Cheapest printing, every time. A card that exists as a normal and a reverse holo is counted at whichever is cheaper, because that is what somebody finishing a set would buy. It makes these totals lower than the price the same card shows on our card pages, which quote a specific printing.
  • Before shipping, and on a base set that is the whole bill. Singles come from many sellers, so a real completion pays postage over and over. Add up the cheapest listing for every card in a base set and it comes to $85 on average, against $136 at market. The cards are close to free. What you are actually buying is 138 separate envelopes, which is why people do this in bulk lots from one seller and not one card at a time.
  • Market price, not the price you will pay, and it cuts both ways. Market is what copies have been selling for. Some cards can be had below it right now from somebody clearing stock, and some cannot be had at all, because the cheap commons in any set are often listed by nobody. So this is a middle estimate rather than a floor or a ceiling: expect to beat it on the cards everyone has and to pay over it on the last few.
  • Reverse holos are not costed. A full reverse-holo run is a separate tier again, and it is a big one. The master-set figures here are one copy of each card number, not one of each printing.
  • No pull rates, no pack math. We have not costed "how many packs to complete this", because it would need pull rates we do not have. Anyone quoting you that number is estimating.

Prices are pricecharting.com's price guide value for an ungraded copy, read August 31, 2026. A guide value is computed across the sales PriceCharting tracks, which is a wider set of venues than any one marketplace. 2 cards of the 5,396 priced here are not listed by PriceCharting and keep their TCGplayer market price via TCGdex instead. The cheapest-listing figures on this page are a different measurement from a different source: they are the lowest live TCGplayer listing via TCGdex, which is one seller's asking price rather than a guide value. Card counts are the published checklists. Totals are computed from those two things and nothing else: no estimates, no rounding up to a nicer number. No affiliate links, and nothing here is a recommendation to buy anything.

The questions this page gets asked

How much does it cost to complete a Pokemon set?

It depends enormously on the set. Across the 29 sets on this page the base set ranges from $20 for Black Bolt to $1,035 for Chilling Reign, buying every card as a single at the cheapest printing and before shipping. The master set, which adds the secret rares, is several times more: Ascended Heroes is $5,564.

Is it cheaper to buy singles or open packs to complete a set?

Singles, almost always, and it is not close. A base set here averages $136 bought as singles. Opening enough packs to see every card in a set costs many times that, because packs repeat commons long before they finish the checklist. Packs are for the fun of opening them; singles are for finishing a set.

What is the difference between a base set and a master set?

The base set is every card numbered up to the printed total, so a card that reads 131/131 is the last one. A master set adds everything numbered above that total, which is where the secret rares and most of the expensive chase cards live. That is why the two numbers are so far apart: in Phantasmal Flames a single card is 58% of the whole master-set bill.
