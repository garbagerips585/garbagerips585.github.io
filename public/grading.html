Is it worth grading?

What it costs, how the five companies compare, and the subtraction almost nobody shows you: what a card is worth raw, what it is worth in a 10, and whether the difference covers the fee.

Home / Grading

Read this first

PSA got a lot pricier this summer

PSA paused its Value and Value Bulk tiers on June 2, 2026. Those were the $24.99 and $32.99 options, and until they return the cheapest way into a PSA slab is $79.99. PSA's own backlog tracker put the active backlog at 11.85 million units on August 11, 2026, down from 12.4 million.

The five

What each one costs

SGC

$15.00 per card, Standard

75+ business days

The cheapest way into a slab from any of the five, and it states no minimum submission, so a single card is fine. The trade is time: 75+ business days is the slowest tier here, and the clock only starts once they have unboxed and logged your order. One caveat we cannot settle: SGC's own page does not say whether there is a per order card limit on this tier, and their submission flow is behind a login, so treat it as unknown rather than unlimited.

Resale. The least established of the five in Pokemon. SGC is long respected in vintage sports cards and its tuxedo slab is distinctive, but an SGC 10 generally sells under a PSA 10 for the same card.

SGC prices →

CGC

$17.00 per card, Bulk

150 days

The cheapest slab with a turnaround under three months. Bulk needs 25 cards in one submission; Economy at $20 has no minimum. SGC is cheaper still at $15 and has no minimum either, but it is the slowest of the five.

Resale. Well recognized and steadily growing, but a CGC 10 generally sells under a PSA 10 for the same card.

CGC prices →

Beckett (BGS)

$79.95 per card, Express

15 business days

Beckett has no Economy tier. Its three cheapest tiers, from $14.95 to $34.95, are all SOLD OUT with a notify-me button, so the cheapest slab you can actually order is $79.95.

Resale. Strong for Black Label, mixed otherwise.

Beckett (BGS) prices →

PSA

$79.99 per card, Regular

40 to 50 business days

Resale. The most recognized slab and the easiest to sell, which is why people pay the most to get one. Not automatically the highest price though, and this page's own resale table is the reason to say so: on the cards we could measure, a BGS 10 sold at 1.30x a PSA 10 of the same card.

PSA prices →

TAG

$149 per card, Priority

5 business days

TAG's site carries a standing REGULAR TIERS PAUSED banner. Its whole regular lineup is marked CURRENTLY AT CAPACITY, so the cheapest TAG slab available is $149.

Resale. Improving quickly, but the premium is the least predictable here because the sample of sold TAG slabs is still small.

And the bits nobody quotes you

  • Shipping there and back, $30 to $60 a submission. Fixed per submission, not per card, so it hurts most when you send one or two.
  • Insurance, $1 to $3 per $100 of declared value. Scales with what you send. Skipping it on a four figure card is not a saving.
  • Supplies, About $1 a card. Sleeves, semi rigids and a sturdy box.
  • PSA Collectors Club, $199 a year. Saves $10 to $15 a card. Worth it only if you are sending a lot.

The part that changes the math

Cheap to grade is not cheap

SGC is the cheapest slab you can buy at $15, and a CGC 10 sells for about 0.46x what the same card makes in a PSA 10. A Beckett 10 sells for 1.30x. So the fee is the smaller half of the decision: paying less to grade can cost you more than it saves, and the gap is far bigger than the difference in fees.

And every figure above is the price of a 10. You do not find out which grade you are getting until the card is already back, so treat the whole table as the best case rather than the expected one. We will not guess at the odds of any particular card coming back a 10, because nobody publishes that. What the companies do publish about grade outcomes, and it is less reassuring than it sounds, is on will it grade.

Five things worth knowing

  • Grading does not make a card valuable. It confirms what condition it is already in, and a 9 is a common outcome that is worth far less than a 10.
  • A 9 is usually worth barely more than the raw card, and sometimes less than the raw card plus what you paid to grade it. Price the downside, not just the PSA 10.
  • Straight out of a pack is not the same as mint, and the manufacturer agrees: The Pokemon Company will not exchange a card for minor scratching or printing defects. The number that decides most submissions is front centering, where PSA and CGC both want roughly 55/45 for a 10 while allowing about 75/25 on the back. Check the front, then the back, then the worst corner, before anything else.
  • Fees are per card whatever the grade, so the math has to clear on the likely outcome, not the best one.
  • If the gap between raw and PSA 10 is smaller than the fee, there is no version of this that pays.

All of this assumes a 10. That is the assumption doing the most work here, and it is the one most likely to be wrong. Front centering has to be about 55/45 before PSA or CGC will call a card a 10, one print line is the whole difference between a 9 and a 10 on a holo, and your worst attribute caps the grade no matter how good the rest is. Will it grade? goes through what each company actually publishes, and it is worth ten minutes before you spend the fee.
