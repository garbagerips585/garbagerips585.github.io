What Pokemon cards should I buy?

Nobody explains any of this in the shop, so here it is. Straight answers, real prices, and no words you are supposed to already know.

  • Mini tin $9.99

    the cheap one that still feels like a present

  • Booster Bundle $26.94

    when the fun is opening packs and nothing else

  • Elite Trainer Box $49.99

    the big birthday box with everything in it

Pick your situation

Who is it for?

Find the one that sounds like you. Every price below is what the manufacturer suggests, not what a shop has to charge.

  1. A present for a young kid

    Five, six, seven. They love Pokemon, they have never opened a pack, and they have definitely never played the game. You want the thing that will not confuse them.

    • Get this: My First Battle

      $9.99Pokemon's own shop price • Pokemon Center, read August 17, 2026

      This is the one. It is built for somebody who has never played anything, it works straight out of the box with no rules to learn first, and it is the cheapest thing on this page. A grown up can sit down with them and play it in about ten minutes.

    • Or: Mini tin

      $9.99Pokemon's own shop price • Pokemon Center, read August 17, 2026 • 2 packs inside

      If what they actually want is the opening bit, get this instead. Two packs, a promo card, and a tin they will still be keeping pencils in when they are fifteen.

      Pictured: Prismatic Evolutions Mini Tin [Flareon].

    How the game works

  2. A kid who already collects and just wants to open stuff

    They know what they are doing. They have a binder. What they want is more packs, and everything you hand them that is not a pack is going in a drawer.

    • Get this: Booster Bundle

      $26.94Pokemon's own shop price • Pokemon Center, read August 17, 2026 • 6 packs inside

      Packs and nothing else. No dice they will lose, no board they will not use, no sleeves they will not put on. At the suggested prices a pack in here costs exactly what a pack in the giant thirty six pack box costs, so this is the cheap way to buy packs without committing to a whole box.

      Pictured: Pitch Black Booster Bundle.

    • Or: Three-pack blister

      $13.99Pokemon's own shop price • Pokemon Center, read August 17, 2026 • 3 packs inside

      The half sized version of the same idea, and the one that hangs on a peg where you can actually reach it. It has a promo card showing through the front, so there is one card they can see before you buy it.

      Pictured: Shrouded Fable 3 Pack Blister [Pecharunt].

    See each one opened

  3. Somebody who wants to actually play the game

    A kid whose friends play at school, or a grown up who has decided this year is the year. They need two decks and the rules, not a pile of loose cards.

    • Get this: Battle Academy

      $24.99Pokemon's own shop price • Pokemon Center, read August 17, 2026

      Three finished decks, a board, counters and a rulebook, and not a single booster pack, which is the entire point. You cannot learn this game out of booster packs, and almost everything else on that shelf is booster packs. Two people can open this and be playing in twenty minutes.

    The rules, short version The free official app

  4. A kid who already plays and wants a proper deck

    They have played, they know what a Trainer card does, and the deck they built out of packs is sixty cards of chaos that loses to everything.

    • Get this: League Battle Deck

      $29.99Pokemon's own shop price • Pokemon Center, read August 17, 2026

      A finished sixty card deck, built to be played, with the sleeves, dice and counters to play it. Open it, shuffle it, take it to a league night. Nothing to build and nothing missing.

    • Or: V Battle Deck

      $14.99Pokemon's own shop price • Pokemon Center, read August 17, 2026

      The cheaper one. Smaller box, one big card at the centre of it, and still a real deck rather than a pile you have to finish yourself.

    Shops with a league night Decks people actually play

  5. The big present, one box with everything in it

    Birthday, Christmas, end of a hard term. You want to hand over one box that is obviously A Present and not have to explain what any of it is.

    • Get this: Elite Trainer Box

      $49.99agreed by price references • UnownArchives, read August 17, 2026 • 9 packs inside

      This is the birthday box and it is why everybody keeps saying those three letters at you. Packs to open, a lidded box to keep the cards in afterwards, sleeves so the good ones stop getting bent, dice and counters for when they start playing. It is the only product that covers the opening and the keeping and the playing in one go.

      Pictured: Pitch Black Elite Trainer Box.

    • Or: Pokemon Center Elite Trainer Box

      $59.99Pokemon's own shop price • Pokemon Center, read August 17, 2026 • 11 packs inside

      Pokemon's own version of the same box: a couple of extra packs and a stamped promo card you cannot get in the shop one. It costs a bit more and it has to be ordered rather than carried home, so it is a plan-ahead present rather than a Saturday one.

      Pictured: Pitch Black Pokemon Center Elite Trainer Box (Exclusive).

    What every box should cost

  6. Under fifteen dollars

    A stocking, a party bag, a reward for a decent report card, or just this is what there is this week. Nobody should feel bad about this bracket. It is where most of the fun is anyway.

    • Get this: Mini tin

      $9.99Pokemon's own shop price • Pokemon Center, read August 17, 2026 • 2 packs inside

      The best ten dollars on the shelf. Two packs, a promo card and a tin that survives a school bag. It also stacks, which matters more than you would think when there are four of them.

      Pictured: Prismatic Evolutions Mini Tin [Flareon].

    • Or: Three-pack blister

      $13.99Pokemon's own shop price • Pokemon Center, read August 17, 2026 • 3 packs inside

      A few dollars more buys three packs and a promo card you can see through the front before you pay for it. Per pack it works out a shade above a bundle, and it is the smallest thing on the shelf that still unwraps like a proper present.

      Pictured: Shrouded Fable 3 Pack Blister [Pecharunt].

    What a pack costs across every set

They want one specific card

Then buy that card. Not packs. Somebody already opened the packs, and the card they want is sitting on a shelf somewhere with a price on it, which is a thing you can do in an afternoon instead of a thing you might do in a year.

It is one of the most expensive mistakes in the whole hobby and it gets made with the best intentions every single time.

The words

What those boxes actually are

Smallest to biggest. Nobody is born knowing any of this and the shop is not going to tell you.

  • Loose booster pack

    $4.49 suggested

    A booster pack. One sealed foil packet of cards. This is the atom: nearly everything else on the shelf is a box with some of these inside it, plus something extra, and the whole game of working out what a box is worth is counting the packs.

    One pack with no sleeve, out of a display box, sold singly across a counter. Pictured: Pitch Black Booster Pack.

  • Three-pack blister

    $13.99 suggested, 3 packs inside

    A blister. The plastic-fronted card that hangs on a peg, with packs behind it and usually a promo card and sometimes a coin showing through the front. Nobody outside the hobby has ever used the word blister for this and it is not your fault for not knowing it.

    A hanging card off a peg with three packs, usually a promo and sometimes a coin. Pictured: Shrouded Fable 3 Pack Blister [Pecharunt].

  • Mini tin

    $9.99 suggested, 2 packs inside

    A tin. A small metal one, with packs and a promo inside. Tins come in about four sizes and the small one is the cheap one; the bigger tins hold more and cost more, and they are all just tins.

    A small metal tin with two packs and a promo or a sticker sheet. The cheapest tin there is. Pictured: Prismatic Evolutions Mini Tin [Flareon].

  • Booster Bundle

    $26.94 suggested, 6 packs inside

    A bundle. A sleeve of packs, shrink wrapped, and genuinely nothing else in the box. If somebody says the word bundle at you, this is what they mean.

    A sleeve of booster packs and nothing else. No sleeves, no dice, no promo. Pictured: Pitch Black Booster Bundle.

  • Elite Trainer Box

    $49.99 suggested, 9 packs inside

    An ETB, and here is the thing nobody bothers to tell you: it is a cardboard box with a lid, with packs in it and the bits for looking after cards. Sleeves, dice, counters, a place to put them. That is the whole mystery. Everybody says ETB because saying Elite Trainer Box out loud takes too long.

    The big one. Booster packs, a storage box, sleeves, dice, damage counters and a promo card. Pictured: Pitch Black Elite Trainer Box.

  • Battle Academy

    $24.99 suggested

    The learn to play box, and the only thing here that is a board game rather than a pile of packs. If the goal is to play rather than to open, this is the shelf you want.

    The learn to play box. Three ready made decks, a board, counters and a rulebook. No booster packs at all.

  • Build and Battle Box

    $21.99 suggested, 4 packs inside

    The prerelease box, which is a shop-event thing that also sells normally. It is the one product that is both: a deck you can play with today and packs to open on the way.

    A forty card ready to play deck, a foil promo and a few packs. The prerelease product. Pictured: Pitch Black Build & Battle Box.

  • Booster box

    $161.64 suggested, 36 packs inside

    A booster box, sometimes called a display box, because it is the box a shop opens to fill the shelf you are standing at. Thirty six packs. This is the big one and there is a section below about why it is almost never the right first purchase.

    The full display box a shop opens to fill its shelf. Thirty six packs. Pictured: Pitch Black Booster Box.

That is the shelf. The full list, all 33 kinds of sealed product with the 26 sourced prices between them, is on the MSRP check, how many packs are inside each one is on packs per box, and every one of them opened on camera is on sealed products.

Save your money

Three things not to buy first

This is the part a shop has no reason to tell you and it is where a beginner loses the most money.

  • Do not start with a booster box

    It is the biggest thing on the shelf, so it looks like the serious purchase, and every restock video on the internet is somebody buying one. It is the wrong first buy for almost everybody and the reason is not the one people assume.

    It is not a bulk discount. The arithmetic is below and it is the same price a pack either way, so what the big box actually buys you is thirty six packs at once, opened in one sitting, and then it is over. Two bundles across two months is the same packs and about six weeks more fun.

    Buy one because you want to open thirty six packs on a Friday night. That is a real reason and it is a completely different reason from thinking it is the sensible way to buy.

    $299.99 asked, $161.64 suggested, which is 1.86x. That is one listing, the Mega Evolution—Pitch Black Booster Box at GameStop, read August 15, 2026. It is an example of the sum, not a score for the shop. Read at https://www.gamestop.com/toys-games/trading-cards/products/pokemon-trading-card-game-pitch-black-booster-box/20034821.html

  • Do not grab one pack off the peg by the till

    Here is the surprise. The suggested price of one pack is exactly the same as the price of a pack inside a bundle or inside the giant box. On paper the single pack is not a rip off at all.

    What happens in real life is that the markup lands hardest on the smallest thing, because a few dollars on a peg item is the amount nobody argues about. The one shop listing this site has written down for a single pack, below, was more than double the suggested figure, and it is the worst multiple of any listing on this site.

    So the rule is not that packs are bad. It is that the impulse buy at the counter, with a kid pulling on your sleeve, is where you are least likely to check and most likely to pay double. Walk ten feet to the peg with the three-pack blister on it and check both.

    $9.99 asked, $4.49 suggested, which is 2.22x. That is one listing, the Pitch Black Sleeved Booster Pack at GameStop, read August 15, 2026. It is an example of the sum, not a score for the shop. Read at https://www.gamestop.com/toys-games/trading-cards/products/pokemon-trading-card-game-pitch-black-sleeved-booster-pack/20034824.html

  • Do not buy a mystery box or a repack

    A mystery box, a mystery pack, a repack, a hundred cards with a guaranteed hit: none of these is a Pokemon product. The Pokemon Company does not make them. Somebody bought cards, chose which ones go in, sealed the bag themselves and priced it.

    That is not automatically a scam and some are made by perfectly honest people. It is just that you are no longer buying a sealed product from the company that printed the cards, you are buying a stranger's judgement about what to put in a bag, and there is no way to check it from the outside.

    The test is easy: if you cannot find the exact product in a shop that sells Pokemon, it is somebody's own creation. Every product on this page is one the manufacturer makes and sells itself.

    Spotting fakes and resealed product

The pack sum, done for you

Price divided by packs, at the suggested figures. This is the arithmetic behind everything above, and it is the useful part, because it still works next year when these numbers have moved and it works on a box that is not on this page.

  • $4.49 a pack Sleeved booster pack, $4.49 for 1 pack
  • $5.00 a pack Mini tin, $9.99 for 2 packs
  • $4.66 a pack Three-pack blister, $13.99 for 3 packs
  • $4.49 a pack Booster Bundle, $26.94 for 6 packs
  • $5.55 a pack Elite Trainer Box, $49.99 for 9 packs
  • $4.49 a pack Booster box, $161.64 for 36 packs

Those are the same number. The big box is not a bulk discount, it is the same packs bought all at once. The Elite Trainer Box comes out dearer a pack than either, and that is fine: you are paying for the box, the sleeves, the dice and the counters, which is exactly why it makes a better present than a bigger pile of packs.

The MSRP check has the same sum as a calculator you can type your own shop's price into, and every shop listing this site has written down.

The one thing almost nobody knows

If you take one thing off this page, take this. It is worth more money to you than everything above it.

The big retailers' websites are two things wearing one page. They are the shop, selling the shop's own stock at the shop's own price, and they are also a marketplace, where other companies list their own goods on the same site and set their own prices.

So when the shop's own stock of a box sells out, the page does not go empty. It fills up with other people's listings of the same box, at whatever they feel like asking. The site looks identical. The logo is the same. The price is not.

That is why a box can show up at a genuinely shocking number on a website you have used for years, on a page that looks completely normal. The shop has not put its prices up. That is a stranger's price sitting on the shop's website.

The tell is printed on every listing and almost nobody reads it: look for who it is sold by and who ships it. If either line is a company name you have never heard of, you are buying from that company and not from the shop. The return window is usually shorter too, which is its own warning sign about who is actually on the hook.

What to do about it is boring and it works. Do not pay it. Stock comes back, usually weekly, and the shop's own price comes back with it. A box that is unavailable this week is still a box, and paying several times the suggested price for one because the page had a familiar logo on it is the most common way somebody new to this loses money.

No shop is being accused of anything here. A marketplace is a normal way to run a website and plenty of the sellers on one are perfectly good. The point is only that the price on the page is not always the shop's price, and almost nobody new to this knows to check.

In the shop

An empty shelf does not mean no stock

Cards get stolen, so a lot of shops now keep them behind the counter, in a locked case, or in a back room, and put nothing at all in the aisle where they used to be. Plenty of people walk the empty aisle, decide there is none, and leave.

Ask somebody who works there. That is the whole trick. Ask if there is any Pokemon behind the counter, ask when the truck comes, ask what day they put it out. Nobody is going to laugh at you and most of them will just tell you.

The same goes for a proper card shop, where the good stuff is behind glass by definition. Walking in and saying you have no idea what you are doing and it is for a seven year old is a completely normal thing to say in a card shop, and it gets you better advice than any website including this one.

Playing, not opening

If the point is to play the game

Most of the shelf does not contain a deck you can play with. Packs are loose cards, and a pile of loose cards is not a deck any more than a bucket of Lego is a spaceship. If somebody wants to play, you need a product that has finished decks in it, and there are only a few.

Everything else on this page is for opening and collecting, which is a completely valid thing to want. Just do not buy an Elite Trainer Box for somebody who asked to learn how to play and wonder why they are stuck.

  • My First Battle $9.99
    Two tiny seventeen card decks and a paper mat, meant for somebody who has never played. The cheapest way in.
  • Battle Academy $24.99
    The learn to play box. Three ready made decks, a board, counters and a rulebook. No booster packs at all.
  • V Battle Deck $14.99
    The cheap ready to play deck, built around one big card. Half the price of a League Battle Deck and a smaller box.
  • League Battle Deck $29.99
    A ready to play sixty card deck with the sleeves, dice and counters to play it. Made to be played rather than opened.
  • Build and Battle Box $21.99
    A forty card ready to play deck, a foil promo and a few packs. The prerelease product.

Where the numbers came from

Every price on this page is joined straight out of the same file the MSRP check prints from, so the two pages cannot disagree and the build stops if they ever do. Nothing here is typed in by hand.

MSRP means the manufacturer's suggested retail price. Pokemon Center is The Pokemon Company's own shop selling its own product, so what it asks IS the suggestion, and most of the figures above are its own store prices read on August 17, 2026. The rest are figures two or more independent price references print identically, which is careful people reading listings and agreeing rather than the manufacturer speaking, and the line under each price says which kind it is.

Suggested is the whole point. No shop has to honour it, which is exactly why it is worth knowing before you walk in. Pack counts come from this site's own product research, one sourced count per product, which is what packs per box is built out of.

Nothing on this page says what is inside a pack or how likely anything is to be in it. This site never states pull rates, because The Pokemon Company does not publish them, and a page giving a parent advice is the last place that belongs.
