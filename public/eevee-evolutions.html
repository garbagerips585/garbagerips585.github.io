How do you get every Eeveelution?

Eight of them, and no two work the same way. Three want a stone. Two want friendship and a clock. Two used to want a specific rock in a specific forest and now want a stone instead. One wants a move. Eevee is the only Pokemon whose answer is eight answers, which is why it gets a page.

Eevee and all eight are here, with the artwork, what each one takes, when it was introduced and what its cards are worth. Tap a face to jump straight to it.

Eevee, and the eight it becomes

One species, 8 branches, 19 separate conditions recorded between them. Each one below carries the condition exactly as our source files it, then the plain English version, then what the Pokemon is and what its cards do.

Eevee

#133

Where it comes from

Nothing evolves into Eevee

Eevee is the one you start with and the only one of the nine with nothing above it. Our source records no route into Eevee at all: nothing evolves into it, so every one of the eight below starts from a plain Eevee and the whole question is what you do next.

  • The Evolution Pokemon. Eevee is #133 in the National Pokedex.
  • Normal type. Normal in the video games. There is no Normal type in the card game, so Eevee cards are printed as Colorless. The eleven card types.
  • Generation 1, Kanto. One of the 151 species that generation introduced.
  • Size. 0.3m (1ft 0in) and 6.5kg (14lb).
  • 160 printings across 93 sets. The priciest single Eevee card we hold a price for is $168. Every Eevee card, priced.

Vaporeon

#134

What it takes

Use a Water Stone
  • Use an item

A Water Stone, and nothing else. No level, no friendship, no time of day. The three stone ones are the easy three, and they are easy in exactly the same way: hold the stone, use it on the Eevee, done.

  • The Bubble Jet Pokemon. Vaporeon is #134 in the National Pokedex.
  • Water type. Water in the video games and Water on a card too. The eleven card types.
  • Generation 1, Kanto. One of the 151 species that generation introduced.
  • Size. 1.0m (3ft 3in) and 29.0kg (64lb).
  • 60 printings across 46 sets. The priciest single Vaporeon card we hold a price for is $280. Every Vaporeon card, priced.

Jolteon

#135

What it takes

Use a Thunder Stone
  • Use an item

A Thunder Stone. Same shape as Vaporeon, different stone. Nothing about the Eevee matters, which is why these three are the ones you can plan around.

  • The Lightning Pokemon. Jolteon is #135 in the National Pokedex.
  • Electric type. Electric in the video games. There is no Electric type in the card game, so Jolteon cards are printed as Lightning. The eleven card types.
  • Generation 1, Kanto. One of the 151 species that generation introduced.
  • Size. 0.8m (2ft 7in) and 24.5kg (54lb).
  • 67 printings across 51 sets. The priciest single Jolteon card we hold a price for is $179. Every Jolteon card, priced.

Flareon

#136

What it takes

Use a Fire Stone
  • Use an item

A Fire Stone. That is the last of the three that are one item and no conditions. Between them, Vaporeon, Jolteon and Flareon are the reason people assume every Eeveelution is a stone, and five of the eight are not.

  • The Flame Pokemon. Flareon is #136 in the National Pokedex.
  • Fire type. Fire in the video games and Fire on a card too. The eleven card types.
  • Generation 1, Kanto. One of the 151 species that generation introduced.
  • Size. 0.9m (2ft 11in) and 25.0kg (55lb).
  • 65 printings across 46 sets. The priciest single Flareon card we hold a price for is $182. Every Flareon card, priced.

Espeon

#196

What it takes

Level up+with friendship 160 or higher+during the day
  • Friendship
  • Time of day

The first one that is not an item. Friendship 160 or higher, then a level up, and it has to happen in the daytime. Friendship is not a stat printed anywhere you can read it, which is what makes this the one people get wrong: it is built up by carrying the Pokemon around rather than bought at a counter.

  • The Sun Pokemon. Espeon is #196 in the National Pokedex.
  • Psychic type. Psychic in the video games and Psychic on a card too. The eleven card types.
  • Generation 2, Johto. One of the 100 species that generation introduced.
  • Size. 0.9m (2ft 11in) and 26.5kg (58lb).
  • 68 printings across 45 sets. The priciest single Espeon card we hold a price for is $290. Every Espeon card, priced.

Umbreon

#197

What it takes

Level up+with friendship 160 or higher+at night
  • Friendship
  • Time of day

Exactly Espeon's condition with the clock moved. Friendship 160 or higher, a level up, at night. The pair is deliberate and it is the reason both are famous: one Eevee, one number, and the only difference is what time you press the button. Get the hour wrong and you get the other one.

  • The Moonlight Pokemon. Umbreon is #197 in the National Pokedex.
  • Dark type. Dark in the video games. There is no Dark type in the card game, so Umbreon cards are printed as Darkness. The eleven card types.
  • Generation 2, Johto. One of the 100 species that generation introduced.
  • Size. 1.0m (3ft 3in) and 27.0kg (60lb).
  • 78 printings across 48 sets. The priciest single Umbreon card we hold a price for is $1,283. Every Umbreon card, priced.

Sylveon

The only one with a move condition. It wants a Fairy type move known, and the other half of the condition changed: affection in one set of games, friendship in another. Both are printed above, and the move requirement is in both of them.

The card game had a Fairy type of its own from the Kalos Starter Set in 2013 until Sword and Shield in February 2020, then retired it. That is why a Sylveon card from the middle of that window is a Fairy card and a new one is a Psychic card. The old ones are ordinary cards, not fakes and not misprints.

  • The Intertwining Pokemon. Sylveon is #700 in the National Pokedex.
  • Fairy type. Fairy in the video games. The card game had a Fairy type of its own and retired it, so Sylveon cards are printed as Psychic now. The eleven card types.
  • Generation 6, Kalos. One of the 72 species that generation introduced.
  • Size. 1.0m (3ft 3in) and 23.5kg (52lb).
  • 53 printings across 30 sets. The priciest single Sylveon card we hold a price for is $471. Every Sylveon card, priced.

Why three of them have more than one answer

Three of the eight carry more than one condition, and that is not us hedging. The method genuinely changed between games, and every version of it is printed with the games it belongs to. Leafeon and Glaceon are the extreme case at six apiece: for five sets of games the answer was a particular rock in a particular place, and the place was different every time.

A page that gives you one of those has given most of its readers the wrong instruction. So this one gives you all of them and tells you which games each belongs to.

Where a condition carries no game tag at all, our source records only one method for it. That means the source files one answer, not that the answer is guaranteed identical in every game ever made. The same rule is what stops the full chart writing "Level 16, in Red and Blue" under Charmeleon.
