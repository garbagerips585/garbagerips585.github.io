The 100 most played cards in Standard

Not the most expensive cards. The ones that actually go in decks. Counted one by one across 357 tournament decklists, so the order comes from arithmetic you can check rather than from anybody's opinion.

The format being counted

Format
Standard
Legal marks
HIJ and any newer ones. The mark is in the bottom corner of the card.
Rotated out
G no longer legal.
In force since
March 26, 2026 in Pokemon TCG Live, April 10, 2026 at in-person events.

Source: Pokemon.com, 2026 Pokemon TCG Standard Format Rotation Announcement, read August 16, 2026. Rotation is announced by Pokemon and it changes, so this is correct as of that date rather than forever. Every card here was played in a list drawn through Limitless's own Standard filter for this rotation, so these cards are format legal because of where the lists came from, not because we checked them one at a time.

What is being measured

Cards are ranked by how many of the 357 decklists play at least one copy. Total copies is shown next to it and breaks ties, and so is the number of the 18 different decks a card turns up in. That is the whole method. There is no scoring, no weighting and no judgment in the order. On each row, lists means individual registered decklists and decks means distinct archetypes.

The lists are 357 decklists collected from Limitless TCG on August 16, 2026, dated July 18, 2026 to August 16, 2026: up to 20 of the highest placing lists from each of the 18 most played decks. These are online tournaments played in Pokemon TCG Live on Limitless's own play platform, not Regionals and not the World Championships.

Two things this count is not. Every deck contributes about the same number of lists whatever its share of the field, which is deliberate, because it stops one popular deck's pet card outranking a real staple. It also means this is not a measure of how often you will run into a card across the whole metagame. And because each deck contributes at most 20 lists, cards bunch up near multiples of that number, so the order near any tie is arbitrary: 7 cards sit on 21 lists, and all 7 of them are inside the hundred, so nothing was cut between equals here. The largest bunch below the cut is 44 cards on 20 lists, at ranks 101 to 144. Read the bottom of this list as a group and not as an order.
