Where to buy cards online

10 places to buy Pokemon cards, and what each one actually costs once shipping and buyer fees are counted. Every figure was read off the company's own shipping, help or policy page rather than off a comparison site: 6 are fully sourced and the rest say which number is missing and where to go and look.

One example of why that matters, and it is the reason this page exists in the shape it does. Everybody quotes a $35 free shipping threshold for Amazon. Amazon's own free shipping help page says "the stated minimum threshold of eligible items" six times and never states the number. So this page tells you the rule and sends you to your own cart for the figure, which is annoying and true, rather than confident and possibly wrong.

This is where to sell pointed the other way. Same companies, different set of numbers, and the buyer's protection is usually the bigger one. Prices and policies move: every figure here carries the date it was read.

The two questions that decide how a purchase can go wrong

Who touched the card before you did, and who is holding the money if it turns out to be wrong? Neither one is answered by how well known the site is.

Those are two questions, not one, and at the same venue they have different answers. A marketplace holds the money on every order it processes, which is the only reason a dispute exists at all. It has almost never held the card. The exceptions are the interesting part of this page: TCGplayer Direct and eBay's Authenticity Guarantee both put a third party physically between the seller and you, and they are the only two mechanisms found here that PREVENT a problem rather than pay for it afterwards.

Buyer protection is generally the bigger of the two products a venue sells. Sellers get the smaller one, which is what our selling guide records. What varies most is not whether you are covered but how long you have, and trading cards get a shorter clock than almost any other category at the venues that publish a carve-out.

And every protection listed here is conditional on the payment having gone through the platform. Moving it off does not weaken your protection, it deletes it, and all of these policies say so in their own words.

What an order has to reach before shipping is free

Every venue below states its own threshold on its own card, twelve screens apart, and the number is the first thing a buyer wants. Here they are on one axis, with the four that publish no number at all underneath, because that group is the reason this page exists.

Marketplaces

Thousands of sellers, one catalog. The best prices and the most moving parts, because the card belongs to a stranger and the venue only arbitrates after it arrives.

TCGplayer

Costs sourced

Marketplace for singles, with TCGplayer Direct layered on top. A catalog keyed to card and condition, where thousands of independent stores list against the same card. Direct is the same catalog fulfilled by TCGplayer itself.

Best for. Named singles at every price, including cheap ones, because you arrive searching for a card rather than browsing listings. The deepest Pokemon singles catalog of anywhere here.

  • Direct shipping $3.99, or FREE on US Direct orders over $50 before taxes and fees Applies to. The whole Direct package, however many cards and however many sellers TCGplayer's own wording: your entire order ships for free with TCGplayer Direct, no matter how many cards you buy or how many sellers you buy from with the Direct program. Expedited Direct shipping is currently suspended and carries no published price. THE THRESHOLD MOVES: the article's own change log records it updated on June 6, 2026 and corrected on July 8, 2026.
  • Marketplace shipping, per seller $1.49 minimum on an order under $5 from one seller. At $5 or more the minimum does not apply and the seller's own rate does. Applies to. Each store in your cart, separately The widely quoted free shipping over $5 is NOT a platform rule. Crossing $5 only removes TCGplayer's floor. Only Level 4 sellers may set a shipping rate of $0, and those listings carry a visible Free Shipping on Orders Over $5 badge. The minimum itself changed on May 26, 2026 per the page's change log.
  • Typical Marketplace shipping rate Not published TCGplayer publishes the $1.49 floor and nothing else. Sellers set their own rate within ranges TCGplayer does not publish, and shipping is priced off the largest item in the order rather than the whole package. The worked examples on their page use hypothetical sellers, so no typical rate is recorded here.
  • Direct minimum card price $0.40 Applies to. Any card fulfilled by TCGplayer Direct whose value is below $0.40 Automatically rounded up. It does not apply to ordinary Marketplace listings, so the cheapest commons genuinely cost more through Direct. This is the real cost of the single-package convenience.
  • Buyer service or processing fee None published TCGplayer's fee article lists commission, Pro, Direct, Sync and transaction fees and every one of them is charged to the SELLER. The only buyer-facing mention of a fee anywhere is one sentence in the Terms of Service noting that a displayed price does not include shipping, taxes or processing fees that may be required, with no amount and no conditions attached. Recorded as published-absent rather than proven-absent: nobody here reached a live checkout total.
  • TCGplayer Subscription $8.99 a month Applies to. Optional, US only, monthly billing, cancel any time Removes the $50 Direct threshold entirely: free tracked shipping on any Direct order with no minimum. It does nothing for Marketplace shipping, where you still pay each seller's rate. Also earns store credit on orders of $35 or more before shipping and tax, at 1% rising to 3% with account age, capped at $50 per qualifying item.
  • Sales tax on shipping Charged Applies to. The item-level shipping and handling charge as well as the card TCGplayer's own wording: the total selling price of an item will generally include item-level shipping and handling charges. The estimate shown at checkout may differ from what is finally charged.
  • International Direct shipping Canada $7.99, United Kingdom $9.99, Japan $11.99 Applies to. Flat rate per shipment, no matter the order size Direct ships to those three countries only, international orders are never eligible for free shipping, and customs duties are the buyer's responsibility.

How shipping stacks. One flat shipping fee per STORE, in TCGplayer's own words, no matter how many items you order from that store. So the fee scales with the number of sellers in your cart and not with the number of cards. Direct is the escape hatch: it pulls every Direct-qualified card into one package from TCGplayer's own authentication center, at one shipping charge. Direct qualified listings carry an icon, a qualifying order appears as Package #1 in the cart with a blue border, and the Cart Optimizer will rebuild the whole cart three ways, Direct against Verified Sellers against Any Seller, so the totals can be compared before checkout. There is also a Shop Direct filter in the search sidebar, which is not documented in any help article.

If it goes wrong. 30 calendar days after the ESTIMATED delivery date, for every issue type, and the clock runs from the estimate rather than from actual delivery. Singles are otherwise non-returnable, with exactly three exceptions: wrong condition, wrong card, missing card. A seller has 2 business days to respond before you can escalate, and TCGplayer says it monitors the message from the moment you send it, contacts the seller at 24 hours and escalates to its own customer service at 72. On a seller-fault return the SELLER pays return postage and must send a prepaid label or package. Two rules worth knowing: a seller cannot require you to send photos of a condition problem before resolving it, and a seller who sent a more expensive card by mistake cannot charge you to keep it. Refunds are anchored to what you paid, so a price move in either direction does not change them. Damaged in transit: do not open the package. On a Direct order you deal with TCGplayer's own support rather than the seller, and Direct orders cannot be canceled once they enter shipping.

Is it real. TCGplayer Safeguard promises a full refund for any counterfeit card, in those words, and there is one hard condition attached: the suspected counterfeit must physically REACH TCGplayer before a refund is eligible. They send a prepaid label, so you do not pay the postage, but you carry the delay and there is no photo-based route. No dollar threshold, no minimum card value and no stated review turnaround is published. Read the Direct branding carefully: TCGplayer describes the Direct check as making sure you get exactly what you ordered in the right condition, and never uses the word counterfeit about it, so Direct is an accuracy and condition inspection rather than a per-card authenticity certificate. The counterfeit remedy is the same Safeguard claim on Direct and Marketplace alike. Sellers are banned from hedging: listings may not say things like cannot guarantee authenticity.

How it defines condition. TCGplayer publishes the standard the rest of the hobby quotes, and it is the only venue on this page that quantifies it. Five grades, and Mint and Gem Mint are not sold on the marketplace at all. Every imperfection is scored Slight 1, Minor 2, Moderate 4 or Major 8, and the allowable total is 3 points for Near Mint, 6 for Lightly Played, 12 for Moderately Played, 24 for Heavily Played and anything above 24 for Damaged. There are absolute ceilings per flaw as well: Near Mint allows at most 20mm of edgewear and no surface wear or bends at all, and a card with more than 5mm of curl is Damaged whatever else is true of it. Up to 70/30 centering has no effect on condition. What TCGplayer does NOT publish is a percentage of value per grade; the only value figure anywhere is a recommendation that a partial refund of about 10% per condition step is reasonable, which is a refund guideline and not a price guide.

  • Near Mint. Minimal wear from shuffling, play or handling, a nearly unmarked surface, crisp corners and unblemished edges outside a few slight flaws. May have slight edge wear or a couple of minor scratches but looks nearly unplayed with no major defects.
  • Lightly Played. May have minor edge wear, scuffs or scratches. No significant imperfections and no issue with the structural integrity of the card. Noticeable imperfections are acceptable, but none too severe or at too high a volume.
  • Moderately Played. Edge wear, surface wear, scratching or scuffing, bends with creases, or a combination of moderate examples of these. Bends, peels or liquid exposure can still be Moderately Played as long as the structural integrity of the card is intact.
  • Heavily Played. A major amount of wear: a variety of moderate imperfections along with creasing, whitening and other faults. However many imperfections it has, it must still be authenticatable and must not be structurally damaged.
  • Damaged. Wear or imperfections beyond the standards for every other grade: major border wear, scratching or scuffing, bends, tears, splitting, food or liquid, or other damage that affects the integrity of the card. Damaged cards are not eligible for Direct.

Limits and gates.

  • Direct ships to the US, Canada, the UK and Japan only.
  • Damaged cards, and cards with certain manufacturing defects such as miscuts, are not eligible for Direct at all.
  • No minimum order value for Direct eligibility is published anywhere. Four of TCGplayer's own pages were checked and none states one, so none is recorded here.

eBay

Partly sourced

Marketplace. Fixed price and auction, plus eBay Live for livestream selling and breaks

Best for. Graded slabs, vintage, sealed product and anything with a sale history you can look up. The only venue here that inspects an expensive raw card before it reaches you.

  • Shipping Set by the seller, per listing Applies to. Each listing, and each seller There is no eBay-wide shipping rate or free shipping threshold to state, because eBay does not set one. Many card sellers list free shipping and price it into the card instead, which is why the sort that matters is total cost rather than item price.
  • Authenticity Guarantee $0 to the buyer Applies to. Eligible cards $200 and above eBay's own wording: no, eBay covers the cost of authentication and tracked shipping from the authentication facility to you, for a limited time. That last clause is eBay's, not ours, and it is the part to re-check before relying on it.
  • Buyer fee on US trading card purchases UNVERIFIED No eBay page stating a buyer-side fee for US trading card purchases was reached on this check, and no page stating there is none was reached either. Rather than record a zero we could not read, this is left open. The checkout total is the only authority, and it is shown before you commit.

How shipping stacks. Shipping is per listing and per seller, so a multi-seller haul pays every one of them and eBay has no equivalent of TCGplayer Direct. Combined shipping exists but only within a single seller, at that seller's discretion.

If it goes wrong. The eBay Money Back Guarantee, and the trading card carve-out is the number to know. Item not received: report any time after the estimated or actual delivery date and no later than 30 calendar days after it, the seller has 3 business days to respond with tracking or a refund, and you can ask eBay to step in from 3 business days up to 21 business days after the report. Item not as described: 30 calendar days after estimated or actual delivery, and the SELLER pays return postage. But for trading cards specifically, if the seller does not offer returns you must request one no later than 3 CALENDAR DAYS after the estimated or actual delivery date. Every other category gets 30. On a counterfeit claim eBay says you must request a return but may not be required to ship the item back to the seller, which is the opposite of TCGplayer's requirement that the card reach them first. Anything paid for even partly outside eBay is excluded entirely, and so are case breaks, box breaks and pack breaks sold by pre-approved sellers.

Is it real. eBay Authenticity Guarantee is the only mechanism found anywhere that inspects the card BEFORE it reaches the buyer rather than paying out afterwards. Eligible items carry a blue checkmark and go through automatically on purchase: the seller ships to the authentication facility, PSA performs a multi-point inspection of an ungraded card, or inspects the case and label of a graded one for tampering, and the card is then shipped on to you, with signature confirmation required on cards over $1,500. No grading or regrading is performed. In the US it covers individual sports cards, collectible card games, autograph, relic and non-sport cards at $200 and above ungraded, and $200 and above for autograph, relic and other graded cards. Accepted graders are PSA, SGC, CGC Cards and BGS. On an auction it is the final price that decides eligibility, not the starting price.

How it defines condition. Not established. No eBay page defining card condition grades was reached on this check, and eBay's marketplace carries sellers using several different scales. Treat a condition word in an eBay listing title as that seller's opinion rather than a standard, and read the photographs.

Limits and gates.

  • Sets, lots, kits, decks, boxes, packs, art cards and raw redemption cards are NOT eligible for Authenticity Guarantee. It covers single cards only, so no sealed product is ever inspected.
  • The buyer must be in the US and the seller in the US or Canada. Puerto Rico, the US Virgin Islands and armed forces addresses are not covered.
  • Local pickup only listings, and items shipped to PO boxes, are not eligible.
  • On an Authenticity Guarantee card where the seller does not accept returns the sale is final, except that a card damaged in transit can be returned within three days of receiving it.
  • A card the seller throws in free is not covered and may be disposed of.

PARTIAL because the buyer fee question could not be closed on eBay's own pages, and because eBay publishes no card condition definitions that were found. Both are recorded as gaps. eBay's fee pages are written for sellers, and our selling guide records those.

Whatnot

Costs sourced

Live auction marketplace. Livestream shows with in-stream auctions, Buy It Now and breaks, plus a separate always-on marketplace

Best for. Buying in real time and watching the card come out of the pack. The only venue here where the thing you are buying is often being opened on camera as you bid.

  • Shipping, USPS Ground Advantage Up to 4oz $4.47. Over 4oz to 8oz $4.85. Over 8oz to 12oz $5.25. Over 12oz to under 1lb $6.75. 1 to 5lb $7.75 flat regardless of distance. Over 5lb priced on weight and distance. Applies to. Each shipment, and Whatnot picks the service rather than the buyer Whatnot is the only venue here that publishes an actual buyer rate card rather than leaving it to sellers. The quoted prices include their fees. You always see the shipping cost before checkout.
  • Shipping, other services First-Class Mail Letter for eligible cards $0.78 to $1.36 by weight plus fees. Flat Rate for eligible 1 to 70lb packages $12.99 per shipment. Priority Mail priced on weight and distance. Applies to. Whichever service Whatnot selects for the shipment Alaska, Hawaii, US territories and military addresses are billed at standard USPS commercial pricing rather than Whatnot's rates. Packages over one cubic foot are priced on dimensional weight.
  • Seller shipping discounts Free or reduced, at the seller's choice Applies to. Applied automatically at checkout during a show Reduced shipping means you pay up to a stated maximum during the show however many items you buy, and the seller covers the rest.
  • Buyer fee None published Whatnot's fee article covers commission and payment processing and both are charged to the SELLER. No buyer fee or buyer premium article was found in their help center. Recorded as published-absent.
  • Tips Whatever you choose Applies to. Optional, to the seller during a show Worth knowing because tips are explicitly non-refundable under their own terms, so a tip is not part of the purchase and cannot be clawed back with it.

How shipping stacks. Smart Bundling combines multiple purchases from the SAME seller into the cheapest shipment it can, and because 1 to 5lb is a flat $7.75, adding more cards to an existing bundle often costs nothing extra. The limits are the important half: orders can only be bundled if they are from the same seller, and purchases made through different sales channels, such as shows and marketplace, cannot be bundled together. So two wins from the same person in two different places are two shipments and two shipping charges.

If it goes wrong. The Whatnot Buyer Protection Policy covers incomplete or incorrect items, items that are damaged, defective, counterfeit or not matching the condition description, and packages lost, delayed, misdelivered, never shipped or marked delivered and not received. The clock is short and shorter still for cards: the general deadline is the earlier of 30 days from purchase or 14 days from delivery, and for Trading Card Games it is the earlier of 7 DAYS FROM DELIVERY or 30 days from purchase. Whatnot may require the item back before refunding, in the same condition it arrived, so opening a product can reduce or void the refund. Sellers are asked to ship within two business days and most domestic orders arrive in 5 to 14 business days.

Is it real. Counterfeit and inauthentic items are named in the Buyer Protection Policy as a covered issue, on the same clock as everything else, which for trading cards is 7 days from delivery. Whatnot publishes a separate Counterfeit Policy and Restricted Branded Items Policy governing what sellers may list.

How it defines condition. Not established from the pages read. Whatnot's buyer protection covers an item that does not match the condition description, which makes the seller's own stated condition the thing you are protected against, but no Whatnot-published grading scale was found.

Limits and gates.

  • BREAKS ARE PARTLY CARVED OUT, and this is the one to read twice. Whatnot's own wording: any items purchased in a break are only eligible for a refund if an item is missing, delayed, incorrect, or damaged. Being unhappy with what came out is not a covered issue, which is the nature of a break rather than a complaint about Whatnot.
  • Refusing delivery does not qualify an order for a refund, and neither does failing to collect a package held for pickup.
  • An order disputed with your bank is ineligible for a Whatnot refund, so a chargeback closes the platform route.
  • Anything transacted off Whatnot is not eligible for a refund at all.

Online card shops

One company owns the stock, packs it and answers for it. Fewer listings and usually a higher price, and exactly one party to argue with.

CoolStuffInc

CoolStuffInc

Costs sourced

Online card shop. Singles, sealed product and accessories held and shipped by one company, with physical stores behind it

Best for. A singles order you want to arrive in one package from one place, at a shipping threshold low enough that it is often free. The only shop here that publishes both a condition standard and a split shipping rate.

  • Shipping, singles-only orders $2.99 flat, and FREE at $30 or more Applies to. Orders containing only singles, contiguous United States This is the lowest free-shipping bar of any venue on this page.
  • Shipping, standard orders $6.99 flat, and FREE at $150 or more Applies to. Orders containing sealed product or accessories, contiguous United States Adding one sealed item to a singles cart moves the whole order onto this track, which raises the free shipping bar from $30 to $150.
  • Minimum order value None published No minimum appears on the shipping offers page or the conditions of use.
  • Buyer fees None published Checked against the shipping page, the payment options page, the conditions of use and the return policy. Credit and debit, PayPal, checks, money orders and gift certificates are all listed with no fee or surcharge attached. Gift certificates do not count toward the free shipping threshold.
  • Return postage Paid by the buyer, unless the return is their mistake Applies to. All returns Their own wording: customers are responsible for return shipping charges. If the return results from a CoolStuffInc error they reimburse USPS Ground shipping fees after the return arrives.

If it goes wrong. A split window, which is unusual and worth reading: unsealed products within SEVEN days, sealed products and accessories within THIRTY. Items must come back in the condition they were received, and sealed products must still be sealed with the original packaging intact. You have to request a Return Merchandise Number before sending anything, and include it, or the refund does not happen. No restocking fee is mentioned. Two gaps: they state they are not responsible for returns lost or damaged on the way back to them and recommend insuring the parcel, and their pages say nothing about an OUTBOUND package that never arrives.

Is it real. A CONDITION guarantee is published and an AUTHENTICITY guarantee is not, and the difference matters. Their about page says they guarantee that the card condition will meet your expectations or they will take the cards back and refund your money, and describes a strict grading policy plus an in-stock promise that anything listed in stock will actually ship. No counterfeit policy appears on any CoolStuffInc POLICY page. A widely repeated 100% authentic claim traces to their blog and article pages rather than a policy page, one of them dated 2018, so it is not recorded here.

How it defines condition. Their own, in their own words, with no reference to TCGplayer or anyone else. Note it is a three-tier scale rather than the usual five, so their Played band absorbs a range that TCGplayer would split across Lightly and Moderately Played. Do not read across between venues without adjusting for that.

  • Near Mint. May have minor edge wear, surface scuffing or warped foiling, and no other imperfections.
  • Played. May have noticeable edge wear, surface scuffing, binder ring dents, artist signatures or warped foiling. Includes foils bent into a severe U shape. Still tournament playable in sleeves.
  • Heavily Played. Moderate to severe significant edge wear, surface scuffing, binder ring dents, signatures, warped foiling, tears, creases, water damage, or other damage, wear or stains not specified. Tournament viability is not guaranteed at this tier.

Their canonical shipping page is /page/1100. Two other plausible shipping urls were checked and neither carries rates, so there is no second page to contradict this one.

Card Cavern

Card Cavern

Costs sourced

Online card shop, Pokemon specialist. Pokemon singles, sealed product, supplies and a deep catalog of PTCGL digital codes

Best for. Pokemon singles specifically, from somewhere that sells nothing else, and online codes, which are delivered electronically and never shipped at all.

  • Shipping, singles, packs and supplies First-Class Package $3, and FREE if the order total exceeds $20. Priority Mail $8, and FREE if the order total exceeds $100. Applies to. Orders with no sealed product in them Both options include tracking. Priority adds up to $100 of insurance. Plain white envelope shipping has been discontinued for the foreseeable future, which is a buyer-side improvement: an envelope has no tracking and nothing to claim against.
  • Shipping, anything containing sealed product $9.00 up to $99.99, and FREE over $100 Applies to. Sealed product and any mixed order, via USPS Priority Mail Includes tracking and $100 insurance. One sealed item moves the whole order onto this track.
  • The store credit trap Free shipping is computed AFTER credit is applied Applies to. Any order paid with store credit Their own warning: store credit is applied as a discount, so the shipping rates displayed will not show free, as the order total will be $0. A $60 cart paid entirely in credit is a $0 order for threshold purposes.
  • Minimum order value None published Not stated on the shipping policy, the terms of service or the FAQ.
  • Buyer fees None published No fee, surcharge or handling charge appears on the shipping policy or the terms of service.

If it goes wrong. Narrow and specific: no returns or exchanges at all unless you received the wrong product or it arrived in a condition other than what you ordered. Wrong or damaged gets a replacement where possible or a full refund. A condition mismatch gets a replacement where possible or a PARTIAL refund, and their own page says the choice between those is yours, using the example of receiving a Lightly Played card instead of a Near Mint one. Refunds go back to the original payment method after the return is received and inspected, with 24 to 72 hours for the credit to appear. Exchanges need pre-approval by email. Three gaps, all real: no return WINDOW in days is published anywhere, who pays return postage is not stated, and a package that never arrives is not addressed at all.

Is it real. Not established. No authenticity guarantee and no counterfeit statement appears on the condition guide, the returns page, the shipping policy, the terms of service or the FAQ.

How it defines condition. Their own, and they reference no external standard. Only two tiers exist because they stock only two, and their page says so in as many words: they deal only with Near Mint and Lightly Played at this time, and will update the guide if that changes. There is no Moderately Played, Heavily Played or Damaged.

  • Near Mint. Nearly flawless. There may be minor defects including corner or edge nicks, an almost unnoticeable scratch, or a common print error.
  • Lightly Played. May have a few minor defects that are more noticeable, such as light surface scratches, light corner or edge wear, warping especially on foils, or a noticeable print defect including print lines.

Limits and gates.

  • They do not ship outside the United States, and offer no local delivery or in-store pickup.

THE DOMAIN IS cardcaverntradingcards.com. cardcaverntcg.com does not resolve, so a link to it is a dead link. One freshness warning: the holiday closure calendar on their shipping policy still lists 2024 and 2025 dates, so that section of the page is stale even though the rate table reads as current. The rates and the calendar are quoted here separately for that reason, and only the rates are recorded.

The cheapest card is not the cheapest order

On a marketplace the price you compare is per card and the price you pay is per seller. TCGplayer says it plainly on its own shipping page: you are charged one flat shipping fee per store, no matter how many items you order from them. That sentence is usually read as good news, and it is, right up until your cart holds twelve cards from twelve different stores.

  • Twelve cards from one seller is one shipping fee. Twelve cards from twelve sellers is twelve shipping fees, and TCGplayer's floor for an order under $5 from a seller is $1.49, so a cart of twelve cheap commons can pay more in postage than in cards.
  • Crossing $5 with one seller does not make shipping free. It only removes TCGplayer's own minimum, after which the seller's rate applies, and only Level 4 sellers are allowed to set that rate to $0 at all. The listings that do carry a visible badge saying so.
  • TCGplayer Direct is the mechanic built to answer this, and it is the most important thing on this page. Direct orders are pulled from TCGplayer's own authentication center and ship as one package however many sellers are involved, at $3.99, or free over $50. The Cart Optimizer will rebuild your cart as a Direct cart so you can compare the two totals before you buy.
  • Direct is not free of trade-offs. Cards under $0.40 are rounded up to $0.40 when TCGplayer fulfills them, so the cheapest commons genuinely cost more through Direct than through the Marketplace, and damaged cards are not eligible for Direct at all.
  • eBay stacks the same way and has no equivalent: shipping is set per listing by each seller, so a multi-seller haul pays each of them. Combined shipping only helps within one seller.
  • Whatnot bundles automatically, but only within one seller, and its own page says purchases made through different sales channels, such as shows and marketplace, cannot be bundled together. Two wins from the same person in two places are two shipments.

None of this is a reason to avoid a marketplace. It is a reason to sort by price plus shipping rather than by price, which is what TCGplayer's own default sort control is labeled, and to fill a cart from as few sellers as the cards allow.

Who protects a buyer

Buyer protection and seller protection are different products, and the buyer's version is generally the bigger one. What varies most is not whether you are covered, it is how long you have, and trading cards get a shorter clock than almost anything else at the venues that publish a carve-out.

TCGplayer

The clearest published clock of any venue here, and the one that runs from an estimate rather than an event.

Covers.

  • Wrong condition, wrong card, missing card. Singles are otherwise non-returnable, and those three are the whole list.
  • Never arrived, once the estimated delivery date has passed.
  • Damaged in transit, provided you do not open the package. Open it and the seller may refund only what is actually damaged.
  • Counterfeits, at 100%, in TCGplayer's own words.

Clocks.

  • 30 calendar days after the ESTIMATED delivery date, for every issue type. Not from actual delivery, and not from the order date.
  • The seller has 2 business days to respond before you can escalate. TCGplayer says it monitors the message from the moment you send it, contacts the seller at 24 hours and escalates internally at 72.
  • Reported missing on or within 2 business days of the estimated delivery date, the seller may ask you to wait. Later than that, they are expected to refund immediately.

Who inspects the card. Nobody, on an ordinary Marketplace order: the card goes from a stranger's box to yours. On a Direct order TCGplayer pulls it from its own authentication center and checks it, and you deal with TCGplayer support rather than the seller.

Who pays return postage. The seller pays it on a seller-fault return and must send a prepaid label or package. On a counterfeit claim TCGplayer sends the prepaid label itself.

Counterfeits. Refunded in full, with one hard condition: the card must physically reach TCGplayer before a refund is eligible. There is no photo-based route, no published dollar threshold and no published review turnaround.

The buyer carries.

  • Buyer's remorse, a price that moved after you bought, and a sealed item you opened. None of the three is returnable.
  • An address you typed wrong and did not catch before it shipped. Their wording is that no exceptions will be made.
  • Bulk lots, which are sold as is and returnable only if they do not match the seller's own photos and description.

eBay Money Back Guarantee

The widest coverage here and the shortest card-specific deadline here, at the same time.

Covers.

  • The item never arrived, or was not made available for collection.
  • The item does not match the listing: wrong item, or broken, damaged or faulty and not described as such.
  • The seller does not honor the return policy stated in their own listing.

Trading cards specifically. For trading cards, bullion, coins and paper money, if the seller does not offer returns the buyer must request a return no later than 3 CALENDAR DAYS after the estimated or actual delivery date. Every other category gets 30. If the seller does offer returns, their stated window applies instead.

Clocks.

  • Item not received: report any time after the estimated or actual delivery date and no later than 30 calendar days after it.
  • The seller has 3 business days to respond with tracking, an update or a refund.
  • Ask eBay to step in from 3 business days after the report and no later than 21 business days after it. eBay may step in unasked where there is no valid tracking.
  • Not as described, general: 30 calendar days after estimated or actual delivery, or within the seller's own window.

Who pays return postage. If an item is being returned as not as described, the seller is responsible for return shipping.

Counterfeits. Where there are strong indicators the item is counterfeit, the buyer must request a return but may not be required to ship the item back to the seller. That is the opposite of TCGplayer's rule, and it is the more buyer-favorable of the two.

Excluded. Anything where any part of the payment was completed outside eBay: bank transfer, cash, money orders, escrow. Also sports trading card case breaks, box breaks and pack breaks sold by pre-approved sellers, digital content, classified ads and services.

eBay Authenticity Guarantee

The only mechanism found anywhere that inspects a raw card before it reaches the buyer instead of paying out afterwards.

How it works.

  • Eligible listings carry a blue checkmark and go through automatically on purchase. The seller ships to the authentication facility rather than to you.
  • PSA runs a multi-point inspection on an ungraded card, checking corners, edges, surface and overall condition against the listing description. On a graded card they inspect the sealed holder for tampering and verify that the case and label are authentic to the grader.
  • No grading or regrading is performed, so a raw card does not come back with a number on it.
  • The card then ships to you, with signature confirmation required on cards over $1,500.

Threshold. $200 and above for ungraded singles, and $200 and above for autograph, relic and other graded cards. On an auction it is the price it sells for that decides, not the starting price. Accepted graders are PSA, SGC, CGC Cards and BGS.

Only if all of these are true.

  • The buyer must be in the US and the seller in the US or Canada. Puerto Rico, the US Virgin Islands and armed forces addresses are not covered.
  • Single cards only. Sets, lots, kits, decks, boxes, packs, art cards and raw redemption cards are not eligible, so no sealed product is ever inspected.
  • Local pickup only listings, and items shipped to PO boxes, are not eligible.
  • A card the seller gifts in free with the order is not covered and may be disposed of.

Returns. Check the seller's return policy first: if they do not accept returns the sale is final even here. The one exception is transit damage, where you can open a return on a final sale card within three days of receiving it. Items shipping internationally into the US are ineligible for returns.

Excluded. The buyer pays nothing. eBay's own answer to whether there is a fee is no, and it says it covers the cost of authentication and the tracked shipping from the facility to you, for a limited time. That last clause is eBay's own and is the part worth re-checking.

How it works.

  • Nobody holds the money, so there is no claim window, no arbiter and no reversal. There is also nothing to ship, so the whole shipping half of this page does not apply.
  • You inspect the card before the money moves, which is the one thing no online venue offers at any price. What you give up is selection: a shop has what a shop has.

How it actually goes wrong

Each of these is a mechanism rather than a type of person, and the first one is not a scam at all. Every one is defined by who had the card and who had the money at which moment.

  1. The shipping stack. Not a scam and not anybody's fault, which is why it belongs first: it is the most common way a Pokemon order costs more than expected. Marketplace shipping is charged per SELLER, so a cart assembled by picking the cheapest listing for each card pays a separate shipping charge to every one of them. TCGplayer's floor for an order under $5 from a seller is $1.49, so a cart of twelve cheap commons from twelve sellers can spend more on postage than on cards. Sorting by price plus shipping rather than by price is the whole fix, and it is what TCGplayer's own default sort control does. Source read August 15, 2026
  2. The clock that ran out while you were at work. A trading card claim window is measured in days, not weeks, and it starts when the parcel is delivered or when it was estimated to be. eBay gives three calendar days on a not-as-described claim where the seller does not accept returns. Whatnot gives seven from delivery on trading card games. Nothing about either venue changes; the deadline simply passes. Open the package the day it lands and check the card against the listing while the claim is still live. Source read August 15, 2026
  3. The condition word that means two different things. Near Mint is not a standard, it is a word that several companies define separately. TCGplayer scores it as at most 3 points of imperfection with no surface wear and no bends at all. CoolStuffInc runs three tiers where Played absorbs a range TCGplayer would split in two. Card Cavern stocks only Near Mint and Lightly Played and defines both itself. Dave and Adam's prints the words on singles and publishes no definition anywhere. So a card described as Lightly Played at one venue is genuinely not the same object as a card described as Lightly Played at another, and the recourse for a condition mismatch is judged against the seller's own published scale rather than a shared one. All four scales, and the absence of the fourth, were read on the companies' own pages. Source read August 15, 2026
  4. The break you cannot un-open. A break is a bet on an opening, and both live venues carve it out of ordinary buyer protection. eBay excludes case breaks, box breaks and pack breaks sold by pre-approved sellers from the Money Back Guarantee entirely. Whatnot covers a break purchase only if an item is missing, delayed, incorrect or damaged. Neither is a criticism of breaks or of the people running them. No refund policy anywhere can put the cards back in the pack, and both companies say so up front rather than in the small print. Source read August 15, 2026
  5. The final sale nobody read. You buy a sealed box at retail, it arrives crushed, and there is no return. Pokemon Center, Target and Best Buy all publish trading cards as final sale or nonreturnable, and Pokemon Center goes further and states that final sale items cannot be returned for box or outer wrap damage specifically. The practical consequence is that at those three the condition of the outer box on arrival is entirely your risk, and it is worth deciding before you order whether you are buying it to open or to keep. Source read August 15, 2026
  6. The three sellers wearing one storefront. On Amazon the same page can be Amazon's own stock, a third-party seller whose goods Amazon holds and posts, or a third party shipping from their own shelf. Which one you are in decides the shipping, the returns route and whether the A-to-z Guarantee applies at all. Amazon publishes the tell: read the Sold by and Ships from lines under the buy button. Its own pages also warn that a self-shipping third-party seller charges its own shipping fees even when your order clears the free shipping threshold. Source read August 15, 2026
  7. The off-platform payment request. Moving a deal into a message, a bank transfer or a payment app strips protection at every venue read here, and every one of them says so in its own words. eBay lists items paid for even partly outside eBay among the things the Money Back Guarantee does not cover; Whatnot says transactions occurring off Whatnot are not eligible for a refund. It cuts both ways and that is why it gets asked for: a seller asking a buyer to pay off-platform is asking them to give up their refund route in exchange for the seller saving a commission. The request itself is the whole signal. Source read August 15, 2026
  8. The chargeback that closed the door you wanted. Disputing a card order with your bank feels like the strongest move available and at one venue it removes the option you actually wanted. Whatnot's exclusions state that orders disputed with your financial institution are ineligible for a refund from Whatnot. The platform route and the bank route are not additive. Use the platform's own process while its clock is running, and keep the bank as the thing you do after it fails rather than alongside it. Source read August 15, 2026

What actually defends against it

  1. Pay on the platform's own rail. Not advice so much as the precondition for everything else on this page. Every protection listed here is conditional on the payment having gone through the venue. Moving it off does not weaken your protection, it deletes it.
  2. Open the parcel the day it arrives, and photograph it before you do. Two of the clocks here are three days and seven days. Nothing else on this page is defeated as often by ordinary life as a deadline that short. TCGplayer adds a specific rule worth knowing in advance: if a package arrives damaged, do NOT open it, because opening it lets the seller refund only the items actually damaged. Source read August 15, 2026
  3. Sort by price plus shipping, and buy from as few sellers as the cards allow. The single biggest lever on what a marketplace order costs, and it needs no negotiation with anybody. On TCGplayer the Cart Optimizer will rebuild the same cart as a Direct cart, a Verified Sellers cart and an Any Seller cart so the three totals can be compared before you commit. Source read August 15, 2026
  4. On an expensive raw card, let somebody look at it first. These are the only two mechanisms found anywhere that put somebody between the seller and you BEFORE the card ships, rather than paying out after it goes wrong. Everything else on this page is a refund process. eBay Authenticity Guarantee: $200 and above, ungraded singles and graded cards, US buyers, single cards only. The buyer pays nothing for it, and cards over $1,500 arrive with signature confirmation required. TCGplayer Direct: no threshold, but the check is described as accuracy and condition rather than authentication, and Direct is free over $50. Source read August 15, 2026
  5. Read the condition scale of the venue you are on, not the one you know. Near Mint is defined separately by every company that uses it, and one of the four card shops here defines it nowhere at all while still printing it on listings. TCGplayer publishes the most precise scale in the hobby, down to millimeters of edgewear and a points budget per grade, and it is the standard worth learning first even if you shop elsewhere. Source read August 15, 2026
  6. Check the returns rule before you buy sealed, not after it arrives. Three of the retailers here publish trading cards as final sale or nonreturnable, and one of them states that final sale covers box damage in transit. This is a thirty second check that cannot be done afterwards. Source read August 15, 2026
  7. Use the platform process before the bank one. They are not additive. At least one venue here treats an order disputed with your financial institution as ineligible for its own refund, so reaching for the chargeback first can close the route you actually wanted.

Who touched the card before you did

Every venue on this page ends with a package, and the useful way to tell them apart is not how big the company is. It is how many pairs of hands the card passed through, and whether any of them belonged to somebody who checked it. This is the same list ordered by chain length rather than by price.

  • Pokemon Center The Pokemon Company International makes it and ships it. There is no reseller, no marketplace listing and nobody in the middle, which is why no authenticity policy is published: there is nothing for one to protect against. What you trade for the shortest chain is that Pokemon cards are final sale, there is a virtual queue, and purchase limits are set per product.
  • The big-box chains Manufacturer to distributor to retailer to you, all of it sealed. The exposure here is not a counterfeit listing, it is whether the box in your hands is still the box that left the factory. That is a physical inspection question and it lives on our fakes page rather than in any return policy.
  • Amazon Three chains behind one page. Amazon's own stock, a third party whose goods Amazon holds and posts, and a third party shipping off their own shelf. Amazon's own pages describe commingling, where the unit you receive may come from another seller's inventory with both sellers' consent, so on a Ships from Amazon item the chain is genuinely not the one the listing implies.
  • An online card shop One company bought the card, graded it against its own published scale, and packed it. There is exactly one party to argue with, and their scale is the one your condition claim is judged against. Two of the four here publish a scale, one publishes none, and one could not be read at all.
  • TCGplayer Direct The card sits in TCGplayer's authentication center before it ships, gets checked for accuracy and condition, and travels in one package however many sellers it came from. TCGplayer, not the seller, answers for it. Read the wording carefully though: this is an accuracy and condition check, and TCGplayer never calls it a counterfeit check.
  • eBay Authenticity Guarantee The longest chain with the strongest link in it. The seller ships to PSA rather than to you, PSA inspects the card or the slab, and only then does it come to you, signature required over $1,500. It is the only mechanism found anywhere that stops a bad card in transit instead of refunding it afterwards, and the buyer pays nothing. Single cards only, $200 and up, so no sealed product ever goes through it.
  • An ordinary marketplace listing A stranger's box to yours, with nobody looking at the card in between. This is not a criticism and it is where most cards are bought: it is the cheapest chain and the deepest catalog, and the whole point of the protections above is that they exist for exactly this case. Just know that they operate after the card arrives, on a clock, and on trading cards that clock is short.

What the short chain costs you. Selection and price, almost exactly in proportion. The shortest chain sells you one company's own products at list, when it has them. The longest one sells you any card ever printed at whatever the market says today. Nobody sells both.

None of this ranks the venues. A sealed ETB and a $600 raw vintage card are different purchases with different exposures, and the chain that suits one is wrong for the other.

The chain with no links in it at all

Every venue above ends with a package. A counter does not. You look at the card, you hand over money, and the transaction is finished before you leave: nothing to ship, no claim window, nobody to wait on and no clock to miss. This site already publishes 6 shops around Rochester and a card show calendar. What it costs is selection, which is the same trade as everything else on this page.

A counter does not have to be a card shop, either. And before you pay for anything raw and expensive from a stranger, or open a box whose seal looks tired, the checks on real or fake are the ones you can do with the thing in your hands. Resealed packs live there, because a return policy cannot answer a physical question.
