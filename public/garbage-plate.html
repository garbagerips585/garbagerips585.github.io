What is a Garbage Plate?

A Garbage Plate is Rochester, NY's signature dish: two sides, usually macaroni salad and home fries, topped with two hot dogs or burgers, a spiced meat hot sauce, mustard and raw onions, with buttered Italian bread on the side. It comes from Nick Tahou Hots, which owns the name.

It is also the dish this channel is named after. Everything on this page names where it came from, the way this site handles a price, and where something could not be sourced it says so. Looking for somewhere to eat one? All 52 places around Rochester, NY that serve a plate are in the directory.

The Garbage Plate in 30 seconds

What it is
Two sides, usually macaroni salad and home fries, under two hot dogs or two burgers, with a spiced meat sauce poured over everything, then mustard and raw onions. Buttered Italian bread on the side.
Where it is from
Nick Tahou Hots, 320 West Main Street, Rochester, NY, a business that dates itself to 1918.
Why the name
Customers named it by pointing at a plate with all that garbage on it. The family registered GARBAGE PLATE as a federal trademark on August 18, 1992.
Why it has other names
Because of that trademark. Everywhere else sells a trash plate, a junkyard plate, a sloppy plate, a Genny plate or just a plate.
How to order
Pick a meat and two sides and ask for everything, which means hot sauce, mustard and onions.
Where to get one
52 places around Rochester, NY, by area, with hours and prices.

What is actually on one

There is no fixed recipe, which is the first thing to understand about it. A plate is assembled to order out of a short list of parts, and the list below is Nick Tahou Hots' own: every item on it is a box you tick on the order form the restaurant publishes for phone and fax orders.

How to order one

There is no menu language to learn beyond a handful of words, and every one of them is on a real menu somewhere in Rochester, NY. This is what they mean.

Plate
The dish, said on its own. Eater calls it the regional abbreviation, and most menus in town file the whole section under Plates.

Source: Eater, 2017.

Everything
Hot sauce, mustard and onions. Steve T. prints it on the menu, dogTown defines it twice, and Visit Rochester used the same three words to explain it to Fox News. Nobody in any source says "all the way".

Sources: Steve T. menu; dogTown menu; Fox News, 2023.

Hots
Hot dogs. The word is about the dog, not the heat.

Source: Zweigle's FAQ.

Red hot, or Texas hot
An ordinary pork and beef frankfurter. Zweigle's says outright that its red hots are not spicy and that Texas hots is another name for them. On Nick Tahou's menu, Texas means the red.

Sources: Zweigle's FAQ; Tasting Table, 2024.

White hot
Uncured and unsmoked pork, beef and veal, which is why it is pale. It is the Rochester, NY one, and some people here call it a porker.

Source: Zweigle's FAQ.

Hot sauce
A meat sauce, not a bottle of pepper sauce. Some menus just say meat sauce or meat hot sauce.

Sources: Visit Rochester; Tasting Table, 2024.

Half plate, split plate
One meat instead of two, or one of each. Nick Tahou's 2007 menu sold a half plate for a dollar less, and dogTown sells a split plate of one cheeseburger and one dog.

Sources: Nick Tahou menu, 2007; dogTown menu.

On rolls
Buns on the side instead of bread. Nick Tahou's charged a dollar more for it in 2007.

Source: Nick Tahou menu, 2007.

The owner's own order

The owner's own pick, first hand: a cheeseburger plate with all the fixings, because a plain plate misses the point. If you want the part of it you cannot get anywhere else, make it white hots.

Source: Tasting Table, 2024.

What people actually ordered, on the record: Jim Gaffigan had white hots with meat sauce over macaroni salad and home fries, and held the mustard. A Rochester, NY pizza owner told Scripps he gets red hots over mac and potatoes with mustard, onions and Nick's sauce.

Sources: Spectrum News, 2020; Scripps News, 2023.

The hot sauce

The hot sauce is the part that makes it a plate, and it is the part nobody publishes a recipe for. Here is what the people who make it say it is.

  • A Rochester, NY bar owner who makes his own describes the traditional sauce as a bean-less Texas chili with less tomato: ground beef, spices and tomatoes.

    Source: Fox News, 2023.

  • Saveur found paprika and cinnamon in Nick's in 2012, along with plenty of grease and griddle scrapings. Eater placed it somewhere between Tabasco, a briny ragu and Cincinnati's Skyline chili.

    Sources: Saveur, 2012; Eater, 2017.

  • Where Nick's came from, in the family's own words: Alex Tahou has told the story twice, to Saveur in 2012 and to Tasting Table in 2024. His grandfather befriended a Mexican man, gave him work and a place to live, and was repaid with the man's family recipe. Alex says his father handed the recipe to him about four days before he died. That is the family's account, printed as theirs.

    Sources: Tasting Table, 2024; Saveur, 2012.

  • Nick Tahou's sold it by the cup: four ounces for $1.00 in 2007 and $1.25 in 2009. Other kitchens sell theirs to take home. Steve T. does 16 ounce jars, and Fairport Hots sells a quart.

    Sources: Nick Tahou menu, 2007; Nick Tahou menu, 2009; Steve T. menu; Fairport Hots menu.

  • It is not only for plates. Zweigle's calls meat hot sauce, mustard and chopped onion the Rochester, NY tradition for a white hot on a bun.

    Source: Zweigle's on white hots.

Where it came from

Told in the order it happened, with the source for every claim on the claim itself. Two of these are primary: a federal trademark file, and the restaurant's own order form.

  1. 1918

    A hot dog stand on West Main Street

    Nick Tahou Hots dates itself to 1918, and it is the restaurant itself saying so: the front page of its own site carries the line "A Rochester tradition since 1918!" and not much else. The first shop stood a block away, at 296 West Main Street, and the family says it began as a pushcart. What it was called first is reported two ways: West Main Texas Hots in most accounts, and Hots and Potatoes in the Democrat and Chronicle's 2020 piece. Alex Tahou's own 2017 words use Hots and Potatoes, but it is not clear from them whether he meant the shop or the dish.

    Sources: garbageplate.com; Rochester Business Journal, 1995; Tasting Table, 2024; Democrat and Chronicle, 2020; Red Wings, 2017.

  2. Who founded it

    Nick's father founded it, and who invented the plate is still open

    This page used to print this as a coin toss. It is not one anymore. Nick Tahou Hots' own site quotes Wikipedia saying the restaurant "was founded by its namesake, Nick Tahou", but every first-hand family source this page could read says otherwise. Nick told the Rochester Business Journal in 1995 that his father came from Greece shortly after 1900, started the business with a pushcart and died in 1942, which is when Nick took over and put his own name on it. Saveur in 2012, Eater in 2017, the Democrat and Chronicle in 2020 and Tasting Table in 2024 all name that father as Alexander, or Alex, Tahou. So the founder was Alexander, and the site's line is best read as a garbled account of Nick's decades running it. WHO INVENTED THE PLATE is the part that stays open: the 1995 piece credits Nick, and Nick's son Alex credits his grandfather Alexander with what became it. The dish grew over decades, so it probably does not have a single inventor to find.

    Sources: garbageplate.com; Rochester Business Journal, 1995; Saveur, 2012; Eater, 2017; Democrat and Chronicle, 2020; Tasting Table, 2024.

  3. 1942

    Nick takes over and his name goes on the sign

    Nick Tahou followed his father from Greece in the 1930s and took the business over when his father died in 1942. The shop went from West Main Texas Hots to Nick Tahou's Texas Hots, and by the late 1970s to Nick Tahou Hots, Inc. Nick retired around 1990 and handed the counter to his son, also Alex, who still runs it.

    Sources: Rochester Business Journal, 1995; Tasting Table, 2024.

  4. The original

    It was hots and potatoes, and the beans were cold

    The dish did not start as a Garbage Plate and did not start with most of what is on one now. Alex Tahou, the founder's grandson, described the original to Tasting Table in 2024.

    The original plate was hot dogs called hots and potatoes. It was hot dogs, home fries, cold beans, not hot, and two pieces of Italian bread and butter

    Alex Tahou

    Source: Tasting Table, 2024.

  5. 1963 and 1968

    The restaurant moved into a railroad station

    The building at 320 West Main Street was never built to be a restaurant. The Rochester & Genesee Valley Railroad Museum records that its oldest part went up in 1877 as offices for the Pitkin Building and Monumental Steam Stone Works, that the railroad bought it in 1881 and expanded it into a passenger terminal, and that the Buffalo, Rochester & Pittsburgh became part of the Baltimore & Ohio system in 1932. Tahou bought the station in September 1963 for $43,000, and the move down the block waited until the original shop was demolished in 1968.

    Source: Railroad museum, 2021.

  6. 1979 to 2014

    The other Nick Tahou's

    West Main Street was not always the only one. A Nick Tahou Hots II opened on Lyell Avenue in 1979 and became Steve T. Hots & Potatoes in 2007, run by Nick's nephew, which is why that counter still sells the dish under its first name. A third shop opened next to SUNY Oswego in September 1995. And a Henrietta branch at 3070 West Henrietta Road opened on August 3, 2010 and closed in 2014. Today the restaurant's own site lists West Main Street and nothing else.

    Sources: Rochester Business Journal, 1995; Eater, 2017; Democrat and Chronicle, 2010; garbageplate.com.

  7. The name

    College kids named it, and the family fought it

    Nobody at the restaurant chose the word. Students coming out of the bars would point at somebody else's dinner and order it by description, and the description stuck hard enough that the family gave up and put it on the sign. Eater and Alex Tahou himself date that to the 1980s; Tasting Table says the 1970s or 80s. The family did not like it at first, and told the Democrat and Chronicle so in six words: "It's food; it doesn't sound good."

    Guys would come in after drinking, and they'd see somebody that had a plate, have no idea what it was, and just say, give me a plate with all that garbage on it

    Alex Tahou

    Sources: Tasting Table, 2024; Eater, 2017; Red Wings, 2017; Democrat and Chronicle, 2020.

  8. 1991 to 1992

    GARBAGE PLATE became a federal trademark

    This is the part of the story that is written down in a government file rather than retold, and the dates in that file are worth having exactly right. Nick Tahou Hots, Inc. of 320 West Main Street filed for the mark GARBAGE PLATE on July 29, 1991. It registered on August 18, 1992 as registration number 1,708,448 on the Principal Register, in class 29, and it is still live, renewed in 2002, in 2012 and most recently on May 9, 2022. The owner on file is still Nick Tahou Hots, Inc. of 320 West Main Street. One detail the food writing never mentions: the mark covers the prepared dish, in trademark class 29, and not restaurant services.

    prepared entrees consisting primarily of one of the following, hot dogs without buns, hamburgers without buns, steak, pork chops, sausage, ham, fish or eggs and processed potatoes and processed beans

    the registration's own description of the goods

    Source: USPTO trademark record.

  9. Ever since

    Which is why everybody else sells a trash plate

    One restaurant owns the name, so every other kitchen in Rochester, NY serving the same idea calls it something else. Trash plate, Rochester plate, sloppy plate, junkyard plate at dogTown, and at the vegan place, a compost plate. The dish is nobody's property and the two words are, which is the whole reason the menus around here read like a thesaurus. Alex Tahou put the position plainly in 2024.

    We're trademarked, and we're still the only garbage plate

    Alex Tahou

    Sources: Tasting Table, 2024; USPTO trademark record; dogTown menu.

  10. 2017

    The baseball team renamed itself after it

    For one home game on August 10, 2017 the Rochester Red Wings played as the Rochester Plates, in caps and jerseys with a Garbage Plate on them. 13WHAM carried the announcement five weeks out and Spectrum News NY1 filed from the ballpark on the night, which is how this page knows the game was played and not just planned. Their general manager Dan Mason told 13WHAM why, and it is as good a one-line summary of the dish's standing in this city as anybody has managed. It did not stay a one-off. The Plates became every Thursday home game in 2018, and in 2019 the team added a mascot, a garbage can named Mac.

    There is nothing more Rochester than the Garbage Plate. There isn't another food item that Rochesterians are more proud of or that is more celebrated in our city than the Garbage Plate and we can't wait to take the field as the Plates!

    Dan Mason, Red Wings general manager

    Sources: 13WHAM, 2017; Spectrum News, 2017; Red Wings, 2017; Democrat and Chronicle, 2019.

  11. 2021

    The time television said it was closed

    On November 11, 2021 a Today show segment by Andrew Rea, who makes Binging with Babish, said Nick Tahou Hots was closed. It was not. The restaurant said so loudly, called the segment trademark infringement and said the only Garbage Plate is one from its own kitchen. Rea apologized and donated to the restaurant's fundraiser. In 2024 Alex Tahou told Tasting Table he does not object to other kitchens selling their own version, and that the family was trying to license the name to somebody.

    Sources: Democrat and Chronicle, 2021; Tasting Table, 2024.

Every kind of plate

The plate is a template, and every kitchen in town has bent it. These are the versions you can actually order, with where each one is written down.

  • Meatless and vegetarian

    Steve T., Empire Hots and Fairport Hots all sell a meatless plate. Ask about the sauce, because hot sauce is a meat sauce and a meatless plate gets it unless you say otherwise. dogTown swaps in a homemade three-bean veggie chili on its veggie plates, and The Red Fern's Compost Plate is vegan from top to bottom.

    Sources: Steve T. menu; Empire Hots menu; Fairport Hots menu; dogTown veggie menu.

  • Breakfast

    Nick Tahou's sold an egg plate in 2007 and 2009, and opened at 8am when its site still printed hours. Steve T. sells one today, Fairport Hots will put two eggs on any plate, and the directory marks every place with a breakfast plate.

    Sources: Nick Tahou menu, 2007; Steve T. menu; Fairport Hots menu.

  • Fish

    Nick Tahou's fish plate is haddock, and Eater points out who it is for: the Friday crowd in Rochester, NY.

    Sources: Nick Tahou menu, 2007; Eater, 2017.

  • A plate for 25

    Empire Hots sells a build-your-own plate kit for 25 people or more, burgers or hots, mac salad, home fries, meat sauce, onions, bread and butter, at $12 a head.

    Source: Empire Hots menu.

  • The riffs

    In 2021 the Democrat and Chronicle counted nineteen of them around town: a garbage bowl, a stuffed Trash Can burger, a Scratch Plate dessert, what it called a garbage plate candied apple, a healthy trash plate and a greens and beans version. That is a 2021 snapshot, so treat it as history, not a menu.

    Source: Democrat and Chronicle gallery, 2021.

  • At the ballpark

    On the first Plates night in 2017 every concession stand at the stadium made its own: a plate calzone, a Philly plate, a mac plate and a dessert Compost Can of ice cream, brownie, cookie and gummy worms.

    Source: Red Wings, 2017.

  • Outside Rochester

    It travels, a little. Nick Tahou's itself ran a shop in Oswego from 1995. Pittsburgh Magazine found a Red Hot Pile made with Zweigle's in Oakland in 2024, and Westword found a Rochester, NY native selling what it calls garbage plates from a truck in Denver the same year. Both are press reports.

    Sources: Rochester Business Journal, 1995; Pittsburgh Magazine, 2024; Westword, 2024.

Making one at home

Nobody publishes Nick's recipe and this page is not going to pretend to. What it can do is give you the build and the two tips that come from the family itself.

  1. Two sides on the plate. Macaroni salad and home fries is the classic pair; french fries or baked beans are the other two.
  2. The meat on top: two burgers, or two hots split and grilled.
  3. Meat hot sauce spooned over everything, not beside it.
  4. Chopped raw onion and a line of mustard.
  5. Buttered Italian bread on the side.
From the people who make it

For the sauce, start from a hot dog chili: finely ground beef, spices and a little tomato, no beans. For the macaroni salad, Alex Tahou's advice is no salt and no vinegar, just elbows, celery, carrot and plenty of mayonnaise, because vinegar fights the sauce. And for the version his grandfather sold, serve the beans cold.

Sources: Tasting Table, 2024; Fox News, 2023.

Garbage Plate questions

What is a Garbage Plate?

A Rochester, NY dish: two sides, usually macaroni salad and home fries, under two hot dogs or burgers, covered in a spiced meat hot sauce, mustard and raw onions, with buttered bread on the side. It comes from Nick Tahou Hots, which dates itself to 1918.

Sources: Nick Tahou order form; garbageplate.com.

Where can I get a Garbage Plate?

Only Nick Tahou Hots at 320 West Main Street can call it a Garbage Plate, because it owns the trademark. Almost every hots shop, diner and bar in the area sells its own version under another name, and the directory on this site lists the ones confirmed from their own menus.

Source: USPTO trademark record.

Is a Garbage Plate spicy?

Not very, usually. Hot names the dog and the sauce rather than the heat, and Zweigle's says its red hots are not spicy. The sauce is a spiced ground meat sauce, so expect warm spice rather than a pepper burn. Saveur called Nick's mild.

Sources: Zweigle's FAQ; Saveur, 2012.

Is there a vegetarian Garbage Plate?

Yes: Nick Tahou's own order form lists a Veggie Plate. Ask about the sauce, because the hot sauce is a meat sauce. Elsewhere, dogTown uses a three-bean veggie chili on its veggie plates and The Red Fern's Compost Plate is fully vegan. The directory has a filter for meatless plates.

Sources: dogTown veggie menu; Nick Tahou order form.

Why is it called a Garbage Plate?

Customers named it. Students who did not know what the dish was called would ask for a plate with all that garbage on it. The family did not like the name, then registered it as a trademark in 1992.

Sources: Tasting Table, 2024; Democrat and Chronicle, 2020; USPTO trademark record.

When was the Garbage Plate invented?

No source dates the dish itself. The restaurant dates itself to 1918, the name dates to the 1970s or 1980s, and the trademark was registered on August 18, 1992. The dish kept changing for decades: there were no hamburgers on it until the 1950s.

Sources: garbageplate.com; Tasting Table, 2024; USPTO trademark record.

Who invented the Garbage Plate?

It is still argued. Nick Tahou Hots was founded by Alexander Tahou, and his son Nick took it over in 1942. A 1995 interview credits Nick with the plate, and Nick's son Alex credits Alexander with what became it.

Sources: Rochester Business Journal, 1995; Tasting Table, 2024.

Why does nobody else call it a Garbage Plate?

Nick Tahou Hots, Inc. owns GARBAGE PLATE as a federal trademark, registration 1,708,448, renewed most recently on May 9, 2022. So other kitchens sell trash plates, junkyard plates, sloppy plates and so on.

Source: USPTO trademark record.

What does 'everything' mean on a plate?

Hot sauce, mustard and onions. Some places add ketchup if you ask.

Sources: Steve T. menu; dogTown menu; Visit Rochester.

What is a white hot?

A pale Rochester, NY hot dog of uncured, unsmoked pork, beef and veal. Zweigle's is the best known maker. A red hot is an ordinary frankfurter, and on Nick Tahou's menu Texas means the red.

Sources: Zweigle's FAQ; Tasting Table, 2024.

How much does a Garbage Plate cost?

Nick Tahou's publishes no price today. Its own menu said $5.50 for a hot plate in 2007 and $7.00 in 2009, and Scripps reported $12.50 with tax in 2023. Elsewhere, a full plate runs from $9.99 to $22 on the menus read for this site.

Sources: Nick Tahou menu, 2007; Nick Tahou menu, 2009; Scripps News, 2023.

How many calories are in a Garbage Plate?

Nobody has published a real number. No restaurant prints nutrition for one, and Health.com said in 2010 that there is no official analysis before quoting outside guesses of 93 to 203 grams of fat.

Source: Health.com, 2010.

Is Nick Tahou's open late?

It used to be open all night. Its own site publishes no hours now, so call 585-436-0184 before you go. The directory's Open late filter shows the places that print their hours and stay open past 11pm.

Sources: Rochester Business Journal, 1995; garbageplate.com.

Can I buy the hot sauce?

From some kitchens. Steve T. sells 16 ounce jars and Fairport Hots sells it by the quart.

Sources: Steve T. menu; Fairport Hots menu.

What this page does not know

A short sourced history beats a long plausible one. These are the things that get repeated about the Garbage Plate that could not be sourced, so they are not stated above. They are written down here so nobody has to research them twice.

  • WHEN THE FIRST GARBAGE PLATE WAS SERVED. Not the restaurant, the dish. Nothing reachable states a date for it, the accounts that give one give a decade, and the trademark file records when the NAME was filed rather than when the food was first sold. So this page dates the restaurant and the trademark and says openly that the dish's own start date is not documented.
  • THAT THE PLATE IS 100 YEARS OLD. 13WHAM's 2017 story says the Garbage Plate marked its 100th anniversary that year, which would put it at 1917, while the restaurant dates ITSELF to 1918 and the dish was called something else for most of a century. An anniversary of the shop is not an anniversary of the dish and this page does not merge them.
  • CALORIE COUNTS. Numbers between 1,000 and 3,000 are quoted all over the internet for a plate. No restaurant publishes one, and the only source that tried, Health.com in 2010, says in so many words that there is no official dietary analysis and then quotes outside guesses of 93 to 203 grams of fat. The plate is built to order out of four sides and ten meats, so there is no single dish to count, and this site does not print a number it cannot source.
  • WHO INVENTED THE WHITE HOT. Zweigle's is the name on most plate menus, and a Red Wings release in 2025 says Zweigle's first made one in 1925. The Rochester Public Library's local history division says the first white hots came from the Ottman Brothers on Front Street and were already popular by 1921, and Zweigle's own site says only that the white hot began in the 1920s and that it was one of the first to make them commercially. This page does not pick one.
  • HOW MANY PLACES SELL ONE. The Democrat and Chronicle says scores, Tasting Table says dozens, Visit Rochester says the list is long, and nobody gives a number. The directory on this site is the closest thing to a count, and it only counts places whose own menus say so.

Where the photographs came from

Every photograph on this page is somebody else's work under a free license, checked on that file's own page on Wikimedia Commons on August 20, 2026 and re-checked from Commons on every run of scripts/sync-plate-photos.py. Nothing here is hotlinked, cropped or taken from a restaurant, a review site or a search result. The photographer, the license and a link to the license are printed under every picture.

  • The photographer's own description names the restaurant, which is why this is on this card: "Junkyard Plate with hot dogs, macaroni salad, home fries, meat sauce, diced onions, and mustard, from Dogtown in Rochester, New York", and the file's camera position, 43.1439 by -77.5898, is Monroe Avenue at that address. The photographer's title matches the menu: dogTown files the dish under Junkyard Plates, and a second section under Junkyard Dog Plates, read on their own site on August 20, 2026. The individual items under those headings are a Dog Plate, a Cheeseburger Plate, a Split Plate and a Sausage Plate, so the word is the section and not the dish name printed on any one line.
  • The photographer's own description names the restaurant: "Compost Plate at The Red Fern in Rochester, New York", and the file's camera position, 43.1498 by -77.5864, is Oxford Street at that address.
  • WHAT SEVEN OF THE FIRST ELEVEN RESTAURANTS LOOK LIKE. Four of them are on this page in freely licensed photographs and the other seven are not. Bill Gray's joined the three on August 20, 2026, on a second search that went wider than Commons. The other seven were searched a SECOND and then a THIRD time the same day, the third by Wikidata P18, by KartaView and by a re-run of Commons and Openverse against fresh geocodes, and they still have nothing publishable. Those cards carry no picture rather than a borrowed one.
  • WHERE THE SEARCHES LOOKED, so nobody repeats them. Commons was swept four ways on August 20, 2026: a title search per business, an insource: search of every file description, the local categories (Restaurants in Rochester, Fairport, Henrietta, Chili, Gates, Webster, Monroe County) and a GEOSEARCH of a 1,500m circle around all eight addresses. Commons holds exactly two files naming any of the seven and both are Bill Gray's. Openverse was then swept across roughly forty queries with its license filter set to CC BY, CC BY-SA, CC0 and public domain, which is Flickr and Commons together. Chili Hots, Empire Hots, Fairport Hots, Henrietta Hots, Jeremiah's Tavern and Steve T. Hots return NOTHING under a free license anywhere: what exists of them on Flickr is all NonCommercial or NoDerivatives, and neither is usable here. A THIRD SWEEP THE SAME DAY re-ran all of it against fresh geocodes for the seven addresses and added the queries the first two had not made: insource: on each street NUMBER as well as each name, the category tree under Restaurants in New York (state), and a Commons geosearch at 120m, 600m, 1,500m and 10km around every one of the seven. NOT ONE geotagged Commons file sits within 130m of any of them; the closest is a school 132m from Fairport Hots, and the nearest thing to a restaurant is the takeout-carton photograph already rejected below, 1,255m from Henrietta Hots.
  • THE FLICKR SWEEPS HAD A HOLE IN THEM AND IT WAS FOUND AND CLOSED ON AUGUST 20, 2026, and it is the reusable half of this whole exercise. Flickr filters a search by numeric license code, and the codes the first two sweeps passed were 4, 5, 7, 8, 9 and 10. That set OMITS 11 and 12, which are CC BY 4.0 and CC BY-SA 4.0, both free and both in active use, so every earlier "nothing free exists" was measured through a filter that could not see two free licenses. All seven names, all seven street names and a 1km geographic search around all seven addresses were re-run with 11 and 12 added. It still comes back empty. The negative holds, and it now holds for the right reason. IF YOU SWEEP FLICKR AGAIN, PASS 4,5,7,8,9,10,11,12.
  • AND THE SHARPER VERSION OF THE NEGATIVE, which is worth more than "nothing exists" because it says what is actually out there. Photographs of these places DO exist on Flickr and they are not free, read off each result's own license field rather than trusted to a filter: Henrietta Hots was shot by drpep in 2024 and is ALL RIGHTS RESERVED, and Rohrbach has about nine photographs by pearwood, every one of them CC BY-NC-ND. NonCommercial and NoDerivatives both fail here. Three prolific free-license photographers who work this region were identified and cleared one by one: Warren LeMay, who shot Rochester, NY in April 2024 under CC BY-SA 2.0 and photographed downtown landmarks only; Random Retail, who shoots chain retail; and Paul Lowry, whose CC BY 4.0 Rochester, NY food photographs are four of the eleven pictures already on this page and include none of the seven. On Commons the complete upload lists of the region's two most systematic contributors were dumped and searched, 4,529 files by DanielPenfield and 5,000 by Andre Carrotflower, with zero hits. So this is not an absence of searching anymore. These are small suburban businesses that nobody has photographed under a free license.
  • MAPILLARY WAS RE-EXAMINED ON AUGUST 20, 2026 AND HALF OF THE FIRST REJECTION'S REASONING WAS WRONG. This note used to say Mapillary states "no version number anywhere". That was true of mapillary.com/terms and false as a statement about Mapillary. mapillary.com/osm, under the heading "Rights for OpenStreetMap", says in as many words "Mapillary images are available under an open license (CC BY-SA)" and links creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/, and Wikimedia Commons' own Template:Mapillary, which is the template a Commons upload of a Mapillary frame is built from, hardcodes {{cc-by-sa-4.0}} and credits the photographer's USERNAME linked to their Mapillary profile rather than a logo. Both were read on August 20, 2026, the second off the template's own wikitext. So the default license is CC BY-SA 4.0, it carries a version, and a page states it. The sentence that claimed otherwise was a report on ONE page written as a statement about the source, which is the third time that shape of error has been found on this page.
  • WHAT STILL STOPS MAPILLARY, and both halves were checked this time rather than assumed. Section 3 of the terms grants CC BY-SA "unless we indicate otherwise" and names CC BY-NC-SA as a live alternative, and Mapillary's own API documentation lists every field an Image carries (creator, captured_at, geometry, camera_type, the thumb urls and so on) and there is NO license field among them. So the carve-out has nothing per-image to check it against, and that is a gap in what the API publishes rather than a gap in anybody's search. Section 11 then says that an image "downloaded and served from your own servers" must be attributed "by visibly displaying the Mapillary logo and linking back to the Mapillary homepage or corresponding Mapillary image page", which is a condition on top of naming the photographer rather than instead of it. Read on mapillary.com/terms and the developer documentation on August 20, 2026.
  • AND THE PRACTICAL BLOCKER, WHICH IS THE ONE TO FIX FIRST AND IS NOT A LICENSE QUESTION AT ALL. Nothing on Mapillary can be reached from this repo without an API token and this repo holds none: every mapillary.com address answers a generic Meta error page, graph.mapillary.com answers "Invalid OAuth 2.0 Access Token", and tiles.mapillary.com answers 403. That means no frame, no photographer's username and no image page, so there is nothing to fetch and nothing to print a credit from. The coverage this note used to quote (70 frames past Bill Gray's, 50 past Fairport Hots, six each past Chili Hots and Empire Hots) could NOT be re-measured on August 20, 2026 and should not be quoted as current. A token is free to register at mapillary.com/developer; put one in .env and this becomes a real lead again rather than an argument.
  • KARTAVIEW WAS CHECKED THE SAME DAY BECAUSE MAPILLARY COULD NOT BE, and it is the other open street-level library. Its nearby-photos API needs no token, and it does hold Rochester, NY: 101 frames within 300m of downtown. Around the seven it holds nothing that shows a building. The nearest frame to any of them is 312m from Chili Hots and 415m from Jeremiah's Tavern, and Empire Hots, Fairport Hots, Henrietta Hots, Rohrbach and Steve T. Hots have no frame within 600m at all. A frame 300m up the road is a picture of somebody else's building.
