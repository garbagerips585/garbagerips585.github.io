What is a Garbage Plate?

It is the dish this channel is named after, and if you have never had one, here is the whole thing. A Garbage Plate is two sides on the bottom, meat on top of them, a spiced meat sauce poured over the lot, then raw onions and mustard, with bread and butter on the side. It comes from one restaurant in Rochester, New York, it has a federal trademark, and that trademark is why everywhere else in this city sells you a trash plate instead. Nobody eats one with a plan.

Everything on this page names where it came from and the day it was read, which is how the rest of this site handles a price and is the only honest way to handle a history. Where we could not source something, it is not here, and there is a list further down of exactly what that means.

What is actually on one

There is no fixed recipe, which is the first thing to understand about it. A plate is assembled to order out of a short list of parts, and the list below is Nick Tahou Hots' own: every item on it is a box you tick on the order form the restaurant publishes for phone and fax orders.

Where it came from

Told in the order it happened, with the source for every claim on the claim itself. Two of these are primary: a federal trademark file, and the restaurant's own order form.

  1. 1918

    A hot dog stand on West Main Street

    Nick Tahou Hots dates itself to 1918, and it is the restaurant itself saying so: the front page of its own site carries the line "A Rochester tradition since 1918!" and not much else. The shop was called West Main Texas Hots before it was called anything else.

    Source: Nick Tahou Hots, garbageplate.com. Read August 20, 2026.

  2. Who founded it

    The one fact the sources disagree about

    Nick Tahou Hots' own site quotes Wikipedia saying the restaurant "was founded by its namesake, Nick Tahou, who died in 1997 after running the establishment for over 50 years". Wikipedia's article today says something different: that Alexander Tahou, a Greek immigrant, founded it in 1918 and named it for his son Nick. Both cannot be right, and a business quoting an encyclopedia about its own founder is not the same as a business stating it. This page prints the disagreement rather than picking a winner.

    Source: Nick Tahou Hots, garbageplate.com. Read August 20, 2026.

  3. The original

    It was hots and potatoes, and the beans were cold

    The dish did not start as a Garbage Plate and did not start with most of what is on one now. Alex Tahou, the founder's grandson, described the original to Tasting Table in 2024.

    The original plate was hot dogs called hots and potatoes. It was hot dogs, home fries, cold beans, not hot, and two pieces of Italian bread and butter

    Alex Tahou

    Source: Tasting Table, "The Rochester Garbage Plate Is A Delicious Hot Mess", Robyn Song, March 9, 2024. Read August 20, 2026.

  4. 1963 and 1968

    The restaurant moved into a railroad station

    The building at 320 West Main Street was never built to be a restaurant. The Rochester & Genesee Valley Railroad Museum records that its oldest part went up in 1877 as offices for the Pitkin Building and Monumental Steam Stone Works, that the railroad bought it in 1881 and expanded it into a passenger terminal, and that the Buffalo, Rochester & Pittsburgh became part of the Baltimore & Ohio system in 1932. Tahou bought the station in September 1963 for $43,000, and the move down the block waited until the original shop was demolished in 1968.

    Source: Rochester & Genesee Valley Railroad Museum, "Nick Tahou and the BR&P Connection", Otto M. Vondrak, March 4, 2021. Read August 20, 2026.

  5. The name

    College kids named it, and the family fought it

    Nobody at the restaurant chose the word. Students coming out of the bars would point at somebody else's dinner and order it by description, and the description stuck hard enough that the family gave up and put it on the sign.

    Guys would come in after drinking, and they'd see somebody that had a plate, have no idea what it was, and just say, give me a plate with all that garbage on it

    Alex Tahou

    Source: Tasting Table, "The Rochester Garbage Plate Is A Delicious Hot Mess", Robyn Song, March 9, 2024. Read August 20, 2026.

  6. 1991 to 1992

    GARBAGE PLATE became a federal trademark

    This is the part of the story that is written down in a government file rather than retold, and the dates in that file are worth having exactly right. Nick Tahou Hots, Inc. of 320 West Main Street filed for the mark GARBAGE PLATE on July 29, 1991. It registered on August 18, 1992 as registration number 1,708,448 on the Principal Register, in class 029, and it is still live: the record shows the registration has been renewed.

    prepared entrees consisting primarily of one of the following, hot dogs without buns, hamburgers without buns, steak, pork chops, sausage, ham, fish or eggs and processed potatoes and processed beans

    the registration's own description of the goods

    Source: United States Patent and Trademark Office, TSDR record for serial 74189085. Read August 20, 2026.

  7. Ever since

    Which is why everybody else sells a trash plate

    One restaurant owns the name, so every other kitchen in Rochester, NY serving the same idea calls it something else. Trash plate, Rochester plate, sloppy plate, junkyard plate at dogTown, and at the vegan place, a compost plate. The dish is nobody's property and the two words are, which is the whole reason the menus around here read like a thesaurus. Alex Tahou put the position plainly in 2024.

    We're trademarked, and we're still the only garbage plate

    Alex Tahou

    Sources: Tasting Table, "The Rochester Garbage Plate Is A Delicious Hot Mess", Robyn Song, March 9, 2024; United States Patent and Trademark Office, TSDR record for serial 74189085; dogTown Hots, its own menu. Read August 20, 2026.

  8. 2017

    The baseball team renamed itself after it

    For one home game on August 10, 2017 the Rochester Red Wings played as the Rochester Plates, in caps and jerseys with a Garbage Plate on them. 13WHAM carried the announcement five weeks out and Spectrum News NY1 filed from the ballpark on the night, which is how this page knows the game was played and not just planned. Their general manager Dan Mason told 13WHAM why, and it is as good a one line summary of the dish's standing in this city as anybody has managed.

    There is nothing more Rochester than the Garbage Plate. There isn't another food item that Rochesterians are more proud of or that is more celebrated in our city than the Garbage Plate and we can't wait to take the field as the Plates!

    Dan Mason, Red Wings general manager

    Sources: 13WHAM, "Rochester Red Wings to become the Rochester Plates to honor the Garbage Plate", July 4, 2017; Spectrum News NY1, "Red Wings serve up history as Rochester Plates", August 10, 2017. Read August 20, 2026.

What this page does not know

A short sourced history beats a long plausible one. These are the things that get repeated about the Garbage Plate that could not be sourced on August 20, 2026, so they are not stated above. They are written down here rather than left out silently, so nobody has to research them twice.

  • WHEN THE FIRST GARBAGE PLATE WAS SERVED. Not the restaurant, the dish. Nothing reachable states a date for it, the accounts that give one give a decade, and the trademark file records when the NAME was filed rather than when the food was first sold. So this page dates the restaurant and the trademark and says openly that the dish's own start date is not documented.
  • THAT THE PLATE IS 100 YEARS OLD. 13WHAM's 2017 story says the Garbage Plate marked its 100th anniversary that year, which would put it at 1917, while the restaurant dates ITSELF to 1918 and the dish was called something else for most of a century. An anniversary of the shop is not an anniversary of the dish and this page does not merge them.
  • THE HOT SAUCE RECIPE, AND WHERE IT CAME FROM. Several retellings say the meat sauce came from a Mexican friend's family recipe. Every version of that traces back to the same handful of articles rather than to the family, none of the sources reachable here states it first hand, and a recipe's origin is exactly the kind of claim that hardens by repetition. Left out.
  • CALORIE COUNTS. Numbers between 1,000 and 3,000 are quoted all over the internet for a plate. No restaurant publishes one, the plate is built to order out of four possible sides and ten possible meats, so there is no single dish to count, and this site does not print a number it cannot source.
  • EATER'S 2017 FEATURE AND THE DEMOCRAT AND CHRONICLE'S 2020 PIECE. Both are cited by Wikipedia as the source for several of the claims above and neither could be fetched on August 20, 2026, so nothing on this page rests on either. If a later editor can read them, the founder question in the history above is the thing to settle.

  • MAPILLARY WAS RE-EXAMINED ON 20 AUGUST 2026 AND HALF OF THE FIRST REJECTION'S REASONING WAS WRONG. This note used to say Mapillary states "no version number anywhere". That was true of mapillary.com/terms and false as a statement about Mapillary. mapillary.com/osm, under the heading "Rights for OpenStreetMap", says in as many words "Mapillary images are available under an open license (CC BY-SA)" and links creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/, and Wikimedia Commons' own Template:Mapillary, which is the template a Commons upload of a Mapillary frame is built from, hardcodes {{cc-by-sa-4.0}} and credits the photographer's USERNAME linked to their Mapillary profile rather than a logo. Both were read on August 20, 2026, the second off the template's own wikitext. So the default license is CC BY-SA 4.0, it carries a version, and a page states it. The sentence that claimed otherwise was a report on ONE page written as a statement about the source, which is the third time that shape of error has been found on this page.
  • WHAT STILL STOPS MAPILLARY, and both halves were checked this time rather than assumed. Section 3 of the terms grants CC BY-SA "unless we indicate otherwise" and names CC BY-NC-SA as a live alternative, and Mapillary's own API documentation lists every field an Image carries (creator, captured_at, geometry, camera_type, the thumb urls and so on) and there is NO license field among them. So the carve-out has nothing per-image to check it against, and that is a gap in what the API publishes rather than a gap in anybody's search. Section 11 then says that an image "downloaded and served from your own servers" must be attributed "by visibly displaying the Mapillary logo and linking back to the Mapillary homepage or corresponding Mapillary image page", which is a condition on top of naming the photographer rather than instead of it. Read on mapillary.com/terms and the developer documentation on August 20, 2026.
  • AND THE PRACTICAL BLOCKER, WHICH IS THE ONE TO FIX FIRST AND IS NOT A LICENSE QUESTION AT ALL. Nothing on Mapillary can be reached from this repo without an API token and this repo holds none: every mapillary.com address answers a generic Meta error page, graph.mapillary.com answers "Invalid OAuth 2.0 Access Token", and tiles.mapillary.com answers 403. That means no frame, no photographer's username and no image page, so there is nothing to fetch and nothing to print a credit from. The coverage this note used to quote (70 frames past Bill Gray's, 50 past Fairport Hots, six each past Chili Hots and Empire Hots) could NOT be re-measured on August 20, 2026 and should not be quoted as current. A token is free to register at mapillary.com/developer; put one in .env and this becomes a real lead again rather than an argument.
  • KARTAVIEW WAS CHECKED THE SAME DAY BECAUSE MAPILLARY COULD NOT BE, and it is the other open street-level library. Its nearby-photos API needs no token, and it does hold Rochester, NY: 101 frames within 300m of downtown. Around the seven it holds nothing that shows a building. The nearest frame to any of them is 312m from Chili Hots and 415m from Jeremiah's Tavern, and Empire Hots, Fairport Hots, Henrietta Hots, Rohrbach and Steve T. Hots have no frame within 600m at all.
