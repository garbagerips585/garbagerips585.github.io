Where to sell your cards

Every other page here answers what a card is worth. This one answers where you turn that into money, and what each place takes on the way. 14 venues, and the fee figures were read off each company's own page rather than off anybody's blog: 8 are fully sourced and the rest say so.

Prices and fees move. Every figure here carries the date it was read, and if you are about to sell something expensive, check the company's own page before you count on a number.

The question that decides how much a sale can hurt you

Who is holding the money when something goes wrong? It is not which site you are on.

Seller protection and buyer protection are different products, and on every venue read here the seller's version is the smaller one. A marketplace sits in the middle, which is the only reason a dispute exists at all, and every one of them voids that protection the moment payment moves off the platform.

What a $100 sale actually costs

Every venue below states its own rate on its own card, and nothing on this page puts them next to each other. Here they are next to each other, on the same sale, so the ranking is visible before you read fourteen of them. The order is not the order the headline percentages give.

Marketplaces

You list it, a buyer finds it, you ship it. The most money and the most work.

eBay

Fees sourced

Marketplace. Fixed price and auction, plus eBay Live for livestream selling

Best for. Anything with a price history: graded slabs, single high-value cards, sealed product. The per-order fee and the flat percentage make cheap singles hard to move at a profit.

  • Final value fee, Toys & Hobbies > Collectible Card Games, no Store or Starter Store 13.25% up to $7,500 per item, then 2.35% on the portion over $7,500 Applies to. Total amount of the sale: item price, handling, buyer-paid shipping, sales tax and any other applicable fees Non-Sport Trading Cards, Sports Trading Cards and Comic Books & Memorabilia carry the same 13.25%. Most other categories are 13.6%.
  • Final value fee, same category, Basic / Premium / Anchor / Enterprise Store 12.35% up to $2,500 per item, then 2.35% on the portion over $2,500 Applies to. Same total amount of the sale A subscription buys 0.9 points off the rate but moves the band edge down from $7,500 to $2,500, so on a big single card the Store is not automatically cheaper.
  • Per order fee $0.30 on orders of $10.00 or less, $0.40 on orders over $10.00 Applies to. Each order, where an order is any number of items bought by the same buyer at checkout with the same shipping method Charged on top of the percentage, inside the same final value fee.
  • Payment processing None charged separately eBay's own wording: one final value fee, and 'you don't have to worry about third-party payment processing fees'. This is the main structural difference from TCGplayer and Whatnot.
  • Insertion fee First 250 listings free each month, then $0.35 per listing with no Store Applies to. Per listing and per category, non-refundable if the item does not sell Store allocations are larger and cheaper: Starter 250 then $0.30; Basic 250 auction in select categories and 1,000 fixed price all categories, then $0.25; Premium 500 / 10,000 then $0.15 and $0.10; Anchor 1,000 / 25,000 then $0.10 and $0.05. Good 'Til Cancelled listings renew monthly and are charged again each renewal.
  • Store subscription Starter $7.95 a month, or $4.95 a month on yearly renewal. Basic $27.95 / $21.95. Premium $74.95 / $59.95. Anchor $349.95 / $299.95. Enterprise $2,999.95, yearly only. Applies to. Monthly, until canceled Starter Store pays the same 13.25% as no Store at all. The lower 12.35% starts at Basic.
  • Dispute fee $20.00 per dispute Applies to. Each dispute where the seller is found responsible for the disputed amount, for example a chargeback Waived when the transaction is eligible under payment dispute seller protections.
  • International fee 1.65% of the total amount of the sale Applies to. Sales where the delivery address or the buyer's registered address is outside the US Not charged if the item ships through eBay International Shipping.
  • Reserve price $5.00 or 7.5% of the reserve price, whichever is greater, capped at $250 Applies to. Auction listings with a reserve Charged whether or not the item sells.
  • Additional final value fee for seller performance +6% of the applicable final value fees, rising to +7% after four or more consecutive months Applies to. Accounts evaluated Below Standard on the 20th of the month, applied to the following calendar month Separately, a 'Very High' rate of item-not-as-described return requests adds +5%, rising to +6% after four consecutive months. Only one of the two is charged if both apply.
  • eBay Live (livestream selling) 6% commission on the total amount of the sale, capped at $100 per item, plus 2.9% payment processing plus $0.30 per order Applies to. Purchases made during an eBay Live event, and all case break purchases whenever made eBay states this as 8.9% + $0.30, and the 6% cap is reached at $1,667, above which only the 2.9% continues. No insertion fee on Live listings. A promotion through December 31, 2026 drops the per-order fee to $0.10 on orders of $10 or less. Different structure and much lower rate than the same seller's ordinary eBay listings.

Getting paid. Sales proceeds show as Processing, then typically become Available 1 to 2 days after the buyer's payment is confirmed. Payouts default to daily and are initiated about 2 days after payment is confirmed; weekly go out Tuesdays, biweekly every second Tuesday, monthly on the first Tuesday. The bank then typically takes another 1 to 3 business days. Express payout to a verified debit card or eligible bank account arrives in about 30 minutes for a flat $2.00. Must be a checking account or a Visa/Mastercard debit card; a savings account or PayPal cannot receive payouts.

To sell there. An eBay account. A Store is an optional paid subscription and it lowers the rate.

If it goes wrong. Seller protections cover abusive buyer behavior (negative and neutral feedback and defects removed, cases removed from service metrics) and events outside the seller's control such as carrier delays or severe weather when tracking shows on-time shipment. Payment dispute protection, where the transaction is eligible, covers the disputed amount, waives the $20 dispute fee and removes related feedback. The extra Top Rated Seller protections against a false 'not as described' claim, and against an item returned used or damaged, require Top Rated Seller status, US or Canada residence, no 'Very High' service metric, a listing on eBay.com and a 30-day or longer return policy. Protection is lost for false identity, not honoring your own return policy, taking sales off eBay, shipping slower than the service bought, and abuse of the protections themselves.

TCGplayer

Fees sourced

Marketplace. Fixed price singles, with a catalog keyed to card and condition

Best for. Raw singles at every price point, including the cheap ones, because buyers arrive searching for a named card rather than browsing.

  • Marketplace commission, Marketplace Seller (Level 1-4) 10.75% Applies to. Order subtotal Capped at $75 per product sold. This is the rate an ordinary individual seller pays.
  • Transaction fee 2.5% + $0.30 Applies to. Order TOTAL: items + shipping + tax This is the part the headline rate hides. The commission is taken on the subtotal, the transaction fee on the total including what the buyer paid to ship, so buyer-paid shipping is charged even though it is not revenue. International orders are 3.5% + $0.30.
  • Marketplace commission, Pro (non-Direct) or Sync account 9.25% Applies to. Order subtotal, capped at $75 per product Pro accounts additionally pay a Pro fee of 2.5% of subtotal, also capped at $75 per product. Sync accounts (Crystal Commerce, BinderPOS, Ion Sync) pay a Sync fee set entirely by the sync provider instead.
  • Marketplace commission, Direct Seller 8.95% Applies to. Order subtotal, capped at $75 per item 8.95% is the number people quote for TCGplayer and it is the hardest one to actually pay: it comes with the Direct Fee below and is not the ordinary individual seller's rate.
  • Direct Fee Cards under $2.49: 50% of card value. Cards over $2.49: $1.12 per card plus all standard fees. Applies to. Direct orders only, per item For cards under $2.49 the commission, Pro, Sync and transaction fees are omitted and the 50% is the whole charge.
  • International seller PayPal fee 2%, capped at $20.00 Applies to. Non-US sellers paid through PayPal Taken as an account adjustment rather than deducted from the order.

Getting paid. Payments are initiated every Monday and Thursday for sales sitting at 'Completed - Pending Payment', with fees already deducted. A sale reaches that status 24 hours after confirmed delivery, or 24 hours after the expected delivery date if tracking cannot be confirmed or the order shipped without tracking. Direct deposit usually lands about four business days later. Orders shipped outside the US are payable 21 days after the order is marked shipped.

To sell there. A seller account. New sellers start at Level 1 and the level caps how much you can list, not what you pay.

If it goes wrong. TCGplayer's own fee and payment pages state one thing plainly and it cuts against the seller: if an order is under review because of a buyer dispute, the funds are held until the outcome is decided, and if the order was already paid out an equivalent amount is held back from future deposits. No broader seller protection scheme was found on the pages read.

What you are allowed to list, by level.

  • Level 1. Up to 100 items listed at once, $1,000 total value, $500 maximum on any one item, 100 items entered per day To move up: 2 orders that have reached their expected delivery date, and 80% or better feedback
  • Level 2. Up to 500 items, $5,000 total, $1,000 maximum per item, 500 entered per day To move up: 11 orders reaching EDD, and 85% or better feedback
  • Level 3. Up to 50,000 items, $100,000 total, $20,000 maximum per item, no daily entry limit To move up: 51 orders reaching EDD, and 90% or better feedback
  • Level 4. Unlimited listings, plus custom shipping options and pricing import/export

Whatnot

Fees sourced

Live auction marketplace. Livestream shows with in-stream auctions and Buy It Now, plus a marketplace

Best for. Selling in volume in real time: rips, breaks, lots and singles that benefit from being shown on camera.

  • Commission, TCG category (US, Canada, Australia) 8% on the final sale price up to $1,500, and 0% on the portion above $1,500 Applies to. The item's final sale price, EXCLUDING shipping and taxes Whatnot's own page flags the 0%-above-$1,500 band as limited time. The 8% also covers Comics, Sports Singles and Toys & Hobbies. Coins & Money is 4%.
  • Payment processing fee 2.9% + $0.30 per transaction Applies to. Total order value: the item's final sale price plus any buyer-paid shipping plus buyer-paid taxes This is why '8%' is not 8%. Two identical sale prices produce different take-home because the processing fee moves with shipping and tax. Fees are calculated per transaction even when several purchases are later bundled into one shipment, so each per-transaction $0.30 is charged separately.
  • Taxes on fees Varies by location Applies to. The seller fees themselves Applied automatically and reflected in earnings.
  • Listing, storage and management fees None Whatnot charges nothing to create, store or manage listings. Giveaways carry no seller fees at all because the buyer pays nothing; the seller still pays shipping.
  • Free or reduced shipping you absorb Deducted from earnings Applies to. Whatever share of shipping the seller covers Covering shipping also slightly lowers the processing fee, because the buyer pays less and the total order value falls.

Getting paid. For most US sellers earnings become available up to 4 hours after the carrier confirms delivery, then a requested payout reaches the bank in 1 to 2 business days. Sellers eligible for Early Payout get earnings the moment a shipping label is generated instead of waiting for delivery. Shipping methods without standard tracking run on fixed clocks instead: USPS First-Class Mail Letter (plain white envelope) is 10 days after label generation, bring-your-own-label is 7 days after the tracking number is entered.

To sell there. A seller application is required. You must be at least 13, and 13 to 17 needs a parent or guardian's permission and a special application. Most applicants get access within minutes; some categories are gated and need a second approval.

If it goes wrong. Whatnot covers, and does not charge back to the seller: carrier delays after a timely drop-off, packages lost, undelivered or misdelivered by the carrier, and claims it judges to be abusive or fraudulent buyer behavior. It handles chargebacks and payment disputes itself rather than passing them to the seller. The seller is charged back for late or unverified shipment (orders must ship within two business days with valid drop-off evidence such as an in-app scanner photo or a carrier acceptance scan; from March 2, 2026 sellers are responsible for refunds on late US domestic shipments without that confirmation), for failing to fulfill a high-value item, and for refunds arising from policy violations. Coverage is not unlimited: repeated issues cause Whatnot to reassess eligibility and recover refunded amounts across orders.

Mercari

Fees sourced

Marketplace. Fixed price listings, general resale rather than card-specific

Best for. Singles, lots and sealed product aimed at a general audience rather than a card catalog. No card database, so listings are written by hand.

  • Selling fee 10% Applies to. The combined amount of the item price and buyer-paid shipping Effective January 6, 2025. The flattest structure of any venue here: one percentage, no per-order fee, no tier.
  • Payment processing fee None charged to the seller The old buyer-side processing fee of $0.50 + 2.9% was removed on January 6, 2025 for new and updated listings.
  • Instant Pay $3 flat per cash-out Applies to. Optional fast withdrawal to an eligible debit card Limited to $600 a month and once a day.
  • Direct deposit $0, but $2 non-refundable per FAILED request Applies to. Standard bank withdrawal
  • Buyer Protection fee, paid by the buyer 3.6% Applies to. The item price and buyer-paid shipping Introduced on the same day the old buyer fee was removed, January 6 2025. It is not taken from the seller, but it is what the buyer actually spends, and this file records the buyer-paid premium at every other venue for exactly that reason.

Getting paid. Buyers have 72 hours from delivery to rate the order, after which the funds release to the seller's balance. Standard direct deposit is free; Instant Pay to a debit card costs $3.

To sell there. An account. No subscription, no application, free to list.

If it goes wrong. Mercari states up to $200 of Shipping Protection against loss or damage in transit when the item ships on a Mercari prepaid label. It holds and releases funds itself rather than passing payment between buyer and seller, and runs fraud monitoring on transactions. The seller has 3 days to pack and drop off; shipping independently means tracking must be supplied before payment is released.

Sportlots

Sportlots

Fees sourced

Marketplace and auction site. A fixed-price seller store, or a '1, 2, 3, SOLD' auction that ends once a lot goes three days without a higher bid

Best for. Cheap singles in volume. The commission structure is upside down compared with everywhere else and that is the whole point of it.

  • Fixed-price commission, by TOTAL MONTHLY SALES $0.00-$4.99: 75%. $5.00-$24.99: 50%. $25.00-$49.99: 35%. $50.00-$99.99: 27%. $100.00-$199.99: 23%. $200.00-$299.99: 20%. $300.00-$499.99: 18%. $500.00-$999.99: 17%. $1,000.00-$1,499.99: 16%. $1,500 and above: 15%. Applies to. The seller's total sales for the month, not the individual sale THE RATE IS SET BY YOUR MONTH, NOT BY THE CARD. Sell $4 in a month and they take 75% of it. Sell $1,500 and they take 15%. Nothing else in this file works this way.
  • How expensive cards are counted into that table Only 50% of the portion of a sale price between $5 and $15 counts, and only 25% of the portion above $15 Applies to. The figure fed into the monthly fee schedule Their own worked example: a $50 card contributes only $18.75 to the fee schedule, leaving $31.25 of that sale with no fee applied at all. So the headline percentages overstate the take on anything expensive.
  • Listing fee, fixed price None Their own wording: 'If no cards sell, there will be no charges.'
  • Auction listing fee $0.15 per auction listing Applies to. Each auction listing, charged when you list Sunk whether or not the lot draws a bid. Optional extras: highlighting $0.10, category-level featured $1.00, site-level featured $2.50, all charged the same way.
  • Payment processing Paid by the seller Applies to. PayPal and credit card transaction fees on buyer payments The flat portion is spread across every seller involved in a combined transaction. This is the on-top fee that the commission table does not show.
  • Postage Seller keeps 100% of the postage charged Applies to. Shipping you bill the buyer Sellers set their own postage rates, and set them to $999.99 to opt out.
  • Loot rebate 0% to 10%, seller's choice Applies to. An optional rebate the seller offers buyers Entirely optional and set by the seller.

Getting paid. PayPal sellers are paid automatically on the 1st of each month to the registered email address. Credit sellers have earnings added to their Sportlots credit balance, and credit withdrawals are processed by hand and can take up to 7 days. Orders must be filled within 5 days of being paid for.

To sell there. An account with a payment method set up. No subscription fee anywhere in their seller guide.

If it goes wrong. Not established from the pages read.

Consignment and auction

They sell it for you. Worth it on cards where the buyer pool is small and rich.

COMC

COMC

Fees sourced

Consignment marketplace. You mail cards in, COMC images, catalogs, stores and ships them; you set prices and never touch the card again

Best for. Bulk. Hundreds of mid and low value cards you do not want to list, photograph or ship one at a time. Standard and Select cap the list price at $100 a card, so high-value slabs belong on Elite or elsewhere.

Who protects a seller

Seller protection and buyer protection are different products, and on every venue below the seller's version is the smaller one. This is the part people find out about afterwards.

PayPal Goods and Services

The clearest example of the asymmetry, which is why it leads.

Covers.

  • Unauthorized Transaction
  • Item Not Received

Does not cover.

  • Significantly Not as Described. Verbatim from the ineligible list: items where the buyer claims the item you sent is not what was ordered.
  • Personal payments, including anything sent using friends and family.
  • Item Not Received claims the buyer files directly with their card issuer.
  • Physical items handed over in person. PayPal's ineligible list names items delivered in person, so taking Goods and Services at a face to face meet is outside seller protection by PayPal's own terms. That matters here because this page ends by saying an in person sale is the safest one: it is safest in CASH.

The buyer's side differs. The buyer's side of PayPal is a separate policy with its own, longer clocks, which is the asymmetry worth knowing: the person who paid you generally has more time to open a case than you have to respond to one. The exact windows are published on PayPal's buyer protection page rather than the seller one, and are not quoted here because we could not read them cleanly off the page itself.

eBay

Covers.

  • Item not received, where tracking shows delivery to the address given at checkout.
  • Not as described, but only once the seller has already refunded through eBay or won a Money Back Guarantee case of the same type. Photographs are evidence for the bank, not a protection trigger.

Clocks.

  • 5 calendar days to accept or challenge a payment dispute.
  • 2 business days to report a buyer who returned a different or damaged item.
  • Disputed funds may be held up to 90 days.

Cards specifically. For trading cards specifically, if the seller does not offer returns the buyer must request one within 3 calendar days of delivery. Every other category gets 30.

Signature on delivery. $750 including shipping and tax.

Off platform. Any payment completed outside eBay is excluded from the Money Back Guarantee entirely.

Source, Source 2, Source 3, Source 4, read August 15, 2026

eBay Authenticity Guarantee

The only mechanism found anywhere that PREVENTS the card swap rather than paying for it afterwards.

How it works.

  • Eligible cards go to PSA before they reach the buyer, then ship on with a tamper-proof sticker and a QR code.
  • A return goes back to the authenticator, not to the seller. If the returned item is not the same item, not in the same condition, or the sticker has been tampered with, the return is denied and the item goes back to the buyer.

Threshold. $200 and above for ungraded singles, and for autograph, relic and other graded cards. Pokemon singles fall under the collectible card game category.

Signature on delivery. $1,500 and above on the authenticator to buyer leg, which is a different number from eBay's general $750.

Excluded. Local pickup only listings are not eligible.

The seller carries.

Counterfeits. Handled by TCGplayer, and the card must physically reach them before a refund.

Returns. Singles are otherwise non-returnable, with three exceptions: wrong condition, wrong card, missing card.

Whatnot

Covers.

  • Chargebacks and payment disputes, handled by Whatnot rather than the seller.
  • Carrier delays after a timely drop-off, and packages lost or misdelivered by the carrier.
  • Claims involving abusive or fraudulent buyer behavior.
  • Where fault cannot be established, Whatnot says it will cover the refund up to a reasonable limit. An explicit shared-responsibility category, which is unusual.

The seller is billed back for.

Cards specifically. For trading card games the buyer's claim window is the earlier of 7 days from delivery or 30 days from purchase, against 30 and 14 for general categories.

High value items. Liability on a missing high-value card is the full MARKET value of the card, not the seller's earnings on it.

A private cash sale

There is no policy page to cite, and that is the finding.

How it works.

  • Nobody holds the money, so there is no dispute process, no claim window, no arbiter and no reversal.
  • The exposure is symmetric and it ends when both people walk away.

How it actually goes wrong

Each of these is a mechanism rather than a type of person. Every one is defined by who controls the money at which moment.

  1. The not-as-described chargeback. The buyer receives the card, then tells their bank the item was not as described. This runs on card network rules, not the platform's, and eBay says plainly that the final outcome is decided by the buyer's payment institution. PayPal's seller-side product excludes this category outright, which is the single most useful thing a seller can know before choosing how to take payment. Source read August 15, 2026
  2. The empty box or swapped card return. The buyer opens a not-as-described return, then sends back a different card, a damaged card, or nothing. Every venue names this. eBay allows a deduction of up to 50% and requires the seller to report the buyer within 2 business days. eBay Authenticity Guarantee is the only place found where the swap is structurally prevented rather than compensated, because the return reaches the authenticator first. Source read August 15, 2026
  3. Friends and family, which has no dispute path by design. PayPal states that personal payments are not covered by Purchase Protection. That is the whole mechanism: it is not a purchase, so there is nothing to dispute. It cuts both ways, and that is why it gets asked for. A buyer asking a seller to accept it is asking them to save the fee; a seller asking a buyer for it is asking them to give up their refund route. Neither request is neutral. Source read August 15, 2026
  4. The off-platform payment request. Moving the deal to a message, a bank transfer or a payment app strips protection on every venue read here, and all four say so in their own words. eBay also bans sharing a phone number or email before the sale completes, so on that venue the request itself is the tell. Source read August 15, 2026
  5. The fake payment and the fake-check overpayment. A buyer sends a fake payment notification and hopes the item ships before anyone checks, or sends a check for more than the price and asks for the difference back. The FTC's explanation of why the check version works is the part worth printing: banks must make the money available quickly, usually within two days, and it can take weeks for the bank to work out the check was fake. Source read August 15, 2026
  6. The item-not-received claim after delivery. The buyer has the card and says they do not. The one attack where seller protection is genuinely strong on every venue, provided tracking shows delivery to the checkout address and signature was used above the threshold. Source read August 15, 2026

What actually defends against it

The safest way to sell is the one nobody expects

An in-person cash sale is not a weakened online sale. It is a different transaction with a different risk profile.

  • Nothing ships, so the swap, the counterfeit return and the not-received claim cannot happen.
  • Cash cannot be charged back.
  • Both people inspect before the value moves, and the deal completes in one moment rather than over ten days.

What it costs you. You give up the buyer pool and probably the price. A shop buys at a percentage of market because it has to resell.

The FTC's own guidance for people selling online recommends selling to a local buyer you can meet in person and taking cash. Source read August 15, 2026

Where the line actually is. The risk in a local listing is not the venue, it is three things people do inside it.

  • Shipping instead of meeting. A local listing that becomes a mailed parcel takes on every shipping attack while keeping none of the platform protections.
  • Remote payment instead of cash in hand. A payment app notification, a check, a promise of a transfer.
  • No recourse, which is the point rather than a flaw. It is fine when you inspect and complete on the spot, and unrecoverable once the card has gone.

This site already publishes where the counter-and-table venues are: 6 shops around Rochester and 22 upcoming shows. A shop or a show is a busy public room with a proprietor in it, which removes most of the reason people are nervous about meeting a stranger.
