The bigger box is not the cheaper pack

The received wisdom is that the bigger the box the cheaper the pack. Of the 22 sets here that sell a pack both loose and inside something bigger, that holds 11 times and fails 11 times: buying single packs one at a time is the cheapest pack in Journey Together, Surging Sparks and 9 more. It is not a rule that can be applied from the outside: on the 3 cheapest sets here the cheapest pack comes inside a box or a bundle, and on the 2 priciest the cheapest pack is bought loose. The only way to know is to look at the set you are actually buying, and that is what the table below is.

The Elite Trainer Box

What the ETB premium looks like

22 of the 28 sets on this page sell an Elite Trainer Box, and in not one of them is it the cheapest pack. In 12 of the 22 it is the priciest thing on the row. Measured against the cheapest pack in its own set it runs a median of 1.56x.

That gap is not the whole story and this page will not pretend it is. An Elite Trainer Box also carries sleeves, dice, a promo card, dividers and the box itself, and we have no source for what any of that is worth, so we do not subtract a made up number from the price to make the comparison look better. If you want the sleeves, the premium may be the cheapest sleeves you will buy. If you want packs, there is a cheaper way to get them in every set on this page.

Before you buy anything

What this page will not tell you

  • Whether the cards inside are worth the price. That calculation needs pull rates, and The Pokemon Company does not publish them, so nobody quoting you one has them either. What we do have is 299 openings of these sets on camera with the results written down, which is observed and not the same thing.
  • What you will pay at a shop. Market price is what copies have been selling for online. A counter in Rochester or a table at a card show is its own market, sometimes under this and sometimes over, and there is no feed for either.
  • Anything about half boxes, blisters, tins or collection boxes. They are in the sync and on the set guides with their full prices, but their pack counts are either variable or not in our data, so they are not divided here. A per-pack figure over a guessed pack count is worse than no figure.
  • Which set to open. Cheapest is not best and this is not a recommendation. It is a price list.

Prices are TCGplayer market prices, read August 24, 2026. The pack counts are our own per-kind figures for the current era, sourced on how many packs are in it and applied only to sets from the Scarlet & Violet era onward, because they are not what those products held in earlier ones. Every per-pack figure on this page is one of those prices divided by one of those counts, computed when the page was built. No affiliate links, and nothing here is a recommendation to buy anything. Set guides for all 28 of these are under set guides, and what it costs to buy the cards instead of the packs is on cost to complete a set. The same sum for one small 2026 product, singles against boxes, is on the First Partner Illustration Collection guide, which also has the suggested price beside the shelf price.

The price questions this page gets asked

How much does a Pokemon booster pack cost?

It depends far more on the set than on where you buy it. Across the 28 sets priced on this page a pack runs from $4.77 for Perfect Order to $37.92 for Celebrations, a spread of 7.95x for the same object. Those are TCGplayer market prices read on August 24, 2026, divided by the pack count printed on the product.

Is a booster box cheaper per pack than buying single packs?

Not reliably. A single pack bought on its own is the cheapest pack in 11 of the 22 sets here that sell a pack both ways, and a box or bundle is cheaper in 11. It has to be checked set by set rather than assumed: on the 3 cheapest sets here the cheapest pack comes inside a box or a bundle, and on the 2 priciest the cheapest pack is bought loose.

Is an Elite Trainer Box worth it?

Purely as packs, no. Across the 22 sets priced here the Elite Trainer Box is never the cheapest pack in its set, it is the priciest option on the row in 12 of them, and it runs a median of 1.56x the cheapest pack in the same set. It also comes with sleeves, dice, a promo card and the box itself, and we put no price on those, so whether that gap is worth paying is a question about the accessories rather than about the cards.
