What rarity have I got?

Every Pokemon card tells you how rare it is, in one corner, with one small symbol. Once you know where to look it takes about a second. Here is the corner, magnified, on real cards.

First: which corner

This is the part almost every guide online gets wrong, and it is easy to check yourself. The symbol moved from the bottom right to the bottom left in 2017, at Sun and Moon. Not at Scarlet and Violet, which is what you will usually be told.

Bottom right

Base Set, 1999 • XY era and older

If the number and symbol are on the right, you are holding something from 2016 or earlier.

Bottom left

Prismatic Evolutions, 2025 • Sun and Moon onward

On the left, and it is 2017 or newer. Everything below is about these.

Then: count, then color

Modern sets use stars for six different rarities, which sounds impossible until you know the trick. It is two questions, in this order.

1

How many?

One, two or three. Count them before you look at anything else.

2

What color?

Black, silvery white, or gold. Black is the ordinary game rarities, silver is a full art ex, gold is the collector tiers.

3

That is it

Two black stars is a Double Rare. Two gold stars is a Special Illustration Rare. Same count, completely different card.

The shortcut that skips all of it

Look at the card number instead. If the left number is bigger than the right one, like 199/198, it is a secret rare and it is one of the good ones. Cards inside the set total are the ordinary tiers. You can do this across a table without squinting.

The whole ladder

Lowest to highest, as the modern sets print them. One thing worth knowing right away: this is not a price order. Illustration Rares usually sell for less than Ultra Rares, and Special Illustration Rares often beat everything except gold. Rarity ladder and value ladder are two different things.

  • 1

    Common

    One black circle

    Plain card, matte finish, artwork sitting in a window. The tier every checklist has most of.

    Eevee, Prismatic Evolutions

    We pulled one on camera: Bidoof, Pokémon GO

  • 2

    Uncommon

    One black diamond

    Looks the same as a Common to the naked eye. Most Trainer cards land here.

    Mewtwo, Stellar Crown

  • 3

    Rare

    One black star

    In modern sets these are always holo: foil on the artwork window, matte everywhere else.

    Umbreon, Prismatic Evolutions

    We pulled one on camera: Paras, Crown Zenith

  • 4

    Double Rare

    Two black stars

    A Pokemon ex in an ordinary card frame. The everyday ex, and the only high tier that still looks like a normal card.

    Pikachu ex, Prismatic Evolutions

    We pulled one on camera: Mega Darkrai ex, Pitch Black

  • 5

    Ultra Rare worth chasing

    Two silver stars

    Full art. The character fills the whole card, no artwork window, usually textured.

    Gengar ex, Temporal Forces

    We pulled one on camera: Wailord ex, Pitch Black

  • 6

    Illustration Rare worth chasing

    One gold star

    Full art of a normal Pokemon, not an ex, painted into a scene. The "Pokemon in its habitat" look.

    Eevee, Twilight Masquerade

    We pulled one on camera: Ampharos, Chaos Rising

  • 7

    Special Illustration Rare worth chasing

    Two gold stars

    Full art of an ex or a Supporter in a scene, with glitter over it. Usually the most valuable card in a set.

    Umbreon ex, Prismatic Evolutions

    We pulled one on camera: Gladion’s Final Battle, Pitch Black

  • 8

    ACE SPEC Rare

    Its own ACE SPEC banner

    A magenta and cyan Trainer or Special Energy. You are only allowed one in a deck, which is the whole point of them.

    Prime Catcher, Prismatic Evolutions

    We pulled one on camera: Enriching Energy, Surging Sparks

  • 9

    Hyper Rare worth chasing

    Three gold stars

    The whole card is gold, borders and all. Unmistakable across a room.

    Charizard ex, Obsidian Flames

    We pulled one on camera: Walking Wake ex, Prismatic Evolutions

  • 10

    Mega Hyper Rare worth chasing

    One gold sparkle

    Gold etched Mega Evolution ex with artwork drawn for it rather than a gold recolor of an existing card.

    New to the Mega Evolution era. The symbol is a single four pointed sparkle with a heavy black outline, not three stars: several guides get this wrong.

    Mega Darkrai ex, Pitch Black

    We pulled one on camera: Mega Greninja ex, Chaos Rising

Corners the ladder does not explain

Two names turn up on the set checklists here that are not rungs on the ladder above. One arrived in 2025, the other belongs to the older sets. If you looked up what you pulled and came back with nothing, it is probably one of these.

  • Black White Rare new in 2025

    Two stars, the first filled in and the second an outline

    A full art printed in one color the whole way through. The artwork, the border, the text and the symbol are all black or all white, with no gold anywhere, so it does not read as a top tier card even though it is numbered past the set total. Victini is the odd one out and prints red instead.

    Two stars again, so counting alone will not place it. The tell is that the second star is hollow rather than filled, and that neither star is silver or gold. It is its own thing rather than a rung above Special Illustration Rare, and only these two sets have it: Victini and Zekrom ex in Black Bolt, Victini and Reshiram ex in White Flare.

    Black Bolt and White Flare, July 2025

  • Holo Rare an older name

    One black star, the same symbol a plain Rare carries

    Not a symbol of its own and not a rung of its own. A Holo Rare is a Rare with foil on the artwork window, and the corner prints the same single black star either way. Crown Zenith has both: Entei at 021/159 is the Holo Rare, Bellossom at 003/159 is the plain one, and the two corners are identical.

    It sits exactly where Rare sits on the ladder above. Older checklists split the two and list them as separate lines, which is where the name comes from, so you will meet it on the Sword and Shield set guides here and not on a Scarlet and Violet one. Some checklists print the same tier the other way around, as Rare Holo.

    Sword and Shield sets and older

Shiny, but not rare

Reverse holo is the one that catches everybody. The foil is on everything except the artwork, so the card looks shiny everywhere the picture is not. It does not change the card's rarity at all: a reverse holo Common still prints a black circle and is still a Common. A regular holo is the opposite, foil on the artwork window only.

"Alt art" is not a rarity. It is not printed on any card and it is not in Pokemon's rarity list. It is collector slang, and on a modern card it almost always means an Illustration Rare or a Special Illustration Rare. Check the star color.

Texture is felt rather than printed. Tilt the card under a light and run a fingernail across the art: modern top tier full arts are usually embossed, with the texture following the artwork. It is a good signal, not a rule.

Coming back after a few years

If you collected once and stopped, the single most confusing thing is that "ex" has meant three different things. All three give up two prizes, and they are not the same card.

Pokemon-ex

2003 to 2007, lowercase

The original. No rule box: the rule is plain italic text along the bottom, and abilities are called Poke-POWER or Poke-BODY.

Pokemon-EX

2012 to 2016, uppercase

Black and White through XY. Almost always Basic, whatever the Pokemon's real stage. Mega EX ended your turn unless you played a Spirit Link.

Pokemon ex

2023 to now, lowercase again

Current. Has a bordered rule box in the bottom corner where the Pokedex entry would sit. That box is the quickest way to tell it from a 2003 one.

If what you kept is from 1999, none of the above applies to it. Base Set has no set symbol in the corner, and what decides its value is which of the three print runs it came from: 1st Edition, Shadowless or Unlimited. That is a stamp and a drop shadow rather than a star, and it is on its own page, magnified on a real card the same way these corners are.

Everything that came and went

If you remember any of these, they are all out of print. None of them are coming out of a pack you buy today.

  • Pokemon LV.X

    2007 to 2009

    Played on top of an Active Pokemon. Gold band across the top.

  • Pokemon Prime

    2010

    The word Prime is not printed anywhere on the card. Spot them by the gold name text and spiked artwork edges.

  • Pokemon LEGEND

    2010

    Two card halves that combined into one Pokemon.

  • Pokemon-EX and M Pokemon-EX

    2012 to 2016

    The uppercase EX. Mega EX ended your turn unless you used a Spirit Link.

  • Pokemon BREAK

    2015 to 2016

    Printed sideways and stacked on the stage below.

  • Pokemon-GX

    2017 to 2019

    One GX attack per game. Tag Team GX put two Pokemon on a card and gave up three prizes.

  • Amazing Rare

    2020 to 2021

    A burst of color behind the Pokemon that spills out past the frame, and a rainbow letter A in the corner where the star would be. Nine of them exist, six in Vivid Voltage and three in Shining Fates.

  • Rainbow rares

    2017 to 2022

    Discontinued at Scarlet and Violet. They are not coming out of a modern pack, whatever the glitter on a Special Illustration Rare looks like.

  • V, VMAX and VSTAR

    2020 to January 2023

    Ended at Crown Zenith.

  • Radiant Pokemon

    2022 to 2023

    Always Basic, one per deck, only worth one prize.

What everyone is saying

The words you will hit in the first five minutes of any card video, including ours.

  • IR

    Illustration Rare. One gold star, full art of a non-ex Pokemon in a scene.

  • SIR

    Special Illustration Rare. Two gold stars. Usually the best card in the set.

  • AA / alt art

    Alternate art. Collector slang, not a printed rarity. On a modern card it almost always means an IR or an SIR.

  • FA

    Full art. The illustration covers the whole card with no artwork window.

  • RH

    Reverse holo. Foil on everything except the artwork. Does not change the card's rarity.

  • UR

    Ultra Rare. Two silver stars, a full art ex.

  • Hit

    Any card out of a pack worth meaningfully more than the pack cost.

  • Chase

    The one card everybody is opening the set hoping to find.

  • Bulk

    Commons, uncommons and low value rares. Sold by weight or by the thousand.

  • Slab

    A card sealed in a rigid case by a grading company.

  • Grading

    Sending a card away to be authenticated and scored on condition. PSA runs 1 to 10 and is the market standard for Pokemon; BGS and CGC also grade.

  • Secret rare

    A card numbered higher than the set total, like 199/198.

  • Regulation mark

    The single letter in the bottom corner. It controls tournament legality and has nothing to do with rarity or value.

The seven questions this page gets asked

Where is the rarity symbol on a Pokemon card?

In the bottom corner, next to the card number. On cards from Sun and Moon (2017) onward it is in the bottom left. On XY era cards and older it is in the bottom right.

What do the stars on a Pokemon card mean?

Count them, then check the color. One black star is a Rare, two black stars a Double Rare, two silver stars an Ultra Rare, one gold star an Illustration Rare, two gold stars a Special Illustration Rare and three gold stars a Hyper Rare.

Does a reverse holo make a card rare?

No. Reverse holo is a finish, not a rarity. The foil sits on everything except the artwork, and the card keeps whatever rarity symbol it was printed with. A reverse holo Common is still a Common.

What is an alt art Pokemon card?

Alt art is collector slang rather than a printed rarity. It appears on no card and in no official rarity list. On a modern card it usually means an Illustration Rare or a Special Illustration Rare.

How do I know if a card is a secret rare?

Its collector number is higher than the set total, for example 199/198. Secret rares are the Illustration Rare, Ultra Rare, Special Illustration Rare and Hyper Rare tiers.

What is a Black White Rare Pokemon card?

A rarity introduced with Scarlet and Violet Black Bolt and White Flare in July 2025. The card is a full art printed in a single color, black or white throughout, and the corner prints two stars: the first filled in and the second an outline. Only those two sets have it.

What is the difference between a Rare and a Holo Rare?

The symbol is the same. Both print one black star in the corner. A Holo Rare has foil on the artwork window and a plain Rare does not. Sword and Shield checklists and older list the two separately, which is where the name comes from.
