What have I actually got

Every Pokemon card tells you how rare it is, in one corner, with one small symbol. Once you know where to look it takes about a second. Here is the corner, magnified, on real cards.

First: which corner

This is the bit almost every guide online gets wrong, and it is easy to check yourself. The symbol moved from the bottom right to the bottom left in 2017, at Sun and Moon. Not at Scarlet and Violet, which is what you will usually be told.

Bottom right

Base Set, 1999 • XY era and older

If the number and symbol are on the right, you are holding something from 2016 or earlier.

Bottom left

Prismatic Evolutions, 2025 • Sun and Moon onward

On the left, and it is 2017 or newer. Everything below is about these.

Then: count, then colour

Modern sets use stars for five different rarities, which sounds impossible until you know the trick. It is two questions, in this order.

1

How many?

One, two or three. Count them before you look at anything else.

2

What colour?

Black, silvery white, or gold. Black is the ordinary game rarities, silver is a full art ex, gold is the collector tiers.

3

That is it

Two black stars is a Double Rare. Two gold stars is a Special Illustration Rare. Same count, completely different card.

The shortcut that skips all of it

Look at the card number instead. If the left number is bigger than the right one, like 199/198, it is a secret rare and it is one of the good ones. Cards inside the set total are the ordinary tiers. You can do this across a table without squinting.

The whole ladder

Lowest to highest, as the modern sets print them. One thing worth knowing straight away: this is not a price order. Illustration Rares usually sell for less than Ultra Rares, and Special Illustration Rares often beat everything except gold. Rarity ladder and value ladder are two different things.

  • 1

    Common

    One black circle

    Plain card, matte finish, artwork sitting in a window. The bulk of every pack.

    Eevee, Prismatic Evolutions

  • 2

    Uncommon

    One black diamond

    Looks the same as a Common to the naked eye. Most Trainer cards land here.

    Mewtwo, Stellar Crown

  • 3

    Rare

    One black star

    In modern sets these are always holo: foil on the artwork window, matte everywhere else.

    Umbreon, Prismatic Evolutions

  • 4

    Double Rare

    Two black stars

    A Pokemon ex in an ordinary card frame. The everyday ex, and the only high tier that still looks like a normal card.

    Pikachu ex, Prismatic Evolutions

  • 5

    Ultra Rare worth chasing

    Two silver stars

    Full art. The character fills the whole card, no artwork window, usually textured.

    Gengar ex, Temporal Forces

  • 6

    Illustration Rare worth chasing

    One gold star

    Full art of a normal Pokemon, not an ex, painted into a scene. The "Pokemon in its habitat" look.

    Eevee, Twilight Masquerade

  • 7

    Special Illustration Rare worth chasing

    Two gold stars

    Full art of an ex or a Supporter in a scene, with glitter over it. Usually the most valuable card in a set.

    Umbreon ex, Prismatic Evolutions

  • 8

    ACE SPEC Rare

    Its own ACE SPEC banner

    A magenta and cyan Trainer or Special Energy. You are only allowed one in a deck, which is the whole point of them.

    Prime Catcher, Prismatic Evolutions

  • 9

    Hyper Rare worth chasing

    Three gold stars

    The whole card is gold, borders and all. Unmistakable across a room.

    Charizard ex, Obsidian Flames

  • 10

    Mega Hyper Rare worth chasing

    One gold sparkle

    Gold etched Mega Evolution ex with artwork drawn for it rather than a gold recolour of an existing card.

    New to the Mega Evolution era. The symbol is a single four pointed sparkle with a heavy black outline, not three stars: several guides get this wrong.

    Mega Darkrai ex, Pitch Black

Shiny, but not rare

Reverse holo is the one that catches everybody. The foil is on everything except the artwork, and you get one or two in most packs. It does not change the card's rarity at all: a reverse holo Common still prints a black circle and is still a Common. A regular holo is the opposite, foil on the artwork window only.

"Alt art" is not a rarity. It is not printed on any card and it is not in Pokemon's rarity list. It is collector slang, and on a modern card it almost always means an Illustration Rare or a Special Illustration Rare. Check the star colour.

Texture is felt rather than printed. Tilt the card under a light and run a fingernail across the art: modern top tier full arts are usually embossed, with the texture following the artwork. It is a good signal, not a rule.

Coming back after a few years

If you collected once and stopped, the single most confusing thing is that "ex" has meant three different things. All three give up two prizes, and they are not the same card.

Pokemon-ex

2003 to 2007, lowercase

The original. No rule box: the rule is plain italic text along the bottom, and abilities are called Poke-POWER or Poke-BODY.

Pokemon-EX

2012 to 2016, uppercase

Black and White through XY. Almost always Basic, whatever the Pokemon's real stage. Mega EX ended your turn unless you played a Spirit Link.

Pokemon ex

2023 to now, lowercase again

Current. Has a bordered rule box in the bottom corner where the Pokedex entry would sit. That box is the quickest way to tell it from a 2003 one.

Everything that came and went

If you remember any of these, they are all out of print. None of them are coming out of a pack you buy today.

  • Pokemon LV.X

    2007 to 2009

    Played on top of an Active Pokemon. Gold band across the top.

  • Pokemon Prime

    2010

    The word Prime is not printed anywhere on the card. Spot them by the gold name text and spiked artwork edges.

  • Pokemon LEGEND

    2010

    Two card halves that combined into one Pokemon.

  • Pokemon-EX and M Pokemon-EX

    2012 to 2016

    The uppercase EX. Mega EX ended your turn unless you used a Spirit Link.

  • Pokemon BREAK

    2015 to 2016

    Printed sideways and stacked on the stage below.

  • Pokemon-GX

    2017 to 2019

    One GX attack per game. Tag Team GX put two Pokemon on a card and gave up three prizes.

  • Rainbow rares

    2017 to 2022

    Discontinued at Scarlet and Violet. They are not coming out of a modern pack, whatever the glitter on a Special Illustration Rare looks like.

  • V, VMAX and VSTAR

    2020 to January 2023

    Ended at Crown Zenith.

  • Radiant Pokemon

    2022 to 2023

    Always Basic, one per deck, only worth one prize.

What everyone is saying

The words you will hit in the first five minutes of any card video, including ours.

  • IR

    Illustration Rare. One gold star, full art of a non-ex Pokemon in a scene.

  • SIR

    Special Illustration Rare. Two gold stars. Usually the best card in the set.

  • AA / alt art

    Alternate art. Collector slang, not a printed rarity. On a modern card it almost always means an IR or an SIR.

  • FA

    Full art. The illustration covers the whole card with no artwork window.

  • RH

    Reverse holo. Foil on everything except the artwork. Does not change the card's rarity.

  • UR

    Ultra Rare. Two silver stars, a full art ex.

  • Hit

    Any card out of a pack worth meaningfully more than the pack cost.

  • Chase

    The one card everybody is opening the set hoping to find.

  • Bulk

    Commons, uncommons and low value rares. Sold by weight or by the thousand.

  • Slab

    A card sealed in a rigid case by a grading company.

  • Grading

    Sending a card away to be authenticated and scored on condition. PSA runs 1 to 10 and is the market standard for Pokemon; BGS and CGC also grade.

  • Secret rare

    A card numbered higher than the set total, like 199/198.

  • Regulation mark

    The single letter in the bottom corner. It controls tournament legality and has nothing to do with rarity or value.
