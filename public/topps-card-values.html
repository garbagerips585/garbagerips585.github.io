Topps Pokemon card values: two top 100s

The same 2,701 Topps listings ranked twice, because raw and graded do not give the same answer and it is not close. The most valuable loose Topps card is Dragonite [Tekno] #149; the highest PSA 10 value is Lapras [Sparkle] #131, which sits at number 24 on the raw 100. Both read on August 22, 2026.

What these numbers are

A price guide value, not an auction record

Every figure here is PriceCharting's own price guide, the Ungraded column for the first hundred and the PSA 10 column for the second, read on August 22, 2026. Their published method computes it from completed eBay sales plus their own marketplace, blending the most recent sale, the median, the average and an age weighted average, with outliers and sale dates taken into account. Shipping is excluded. It is an estimate of what a copy is worth right now. It is not a live listing, it is not any one sale, and it is not an appraisal.

These are Topps cards, which are not Pokemon TCG cards. They are trading cards of Pokemon: anime and film stills on card stock, made under license between August 1999 and June 2004, with nothing on them to play a game with. PriceCharting files and prices them under Pokemon, which is why they can be ranked here at all. What they are, every set, and how to spot one is the guide.

87 of the raw hundred and 68 of the PSA 10 hundred are Chrome cards, out of 12 Topps sets. That is not a thumb on the scale, it is what the data says: the two Chrome sets are a small part of the catalog and nearly all of the top of it.

An empty grade cell is an answer, not a gap. 21 rows of the raw hundred carry no PSA 10 figure and 27 carry no Grade 9 figure. This guide prices from completed sales, so a card nobody has sold in that grade recently has no value to report, and a row that could not be confirmed twice says so in place of the number rather than printing one.

What this ranking cannot see. Auction and private sales, which is where a lot of vintage Topps actually changes hands, and any card with no recent recorded sale in the column being ranked. It also cannot see condition: a raw price is a guide value for a loose copy, and the range between a beaten copy and a clean one on cards this old is enormous.

Every figure was read twice before it went on this page. The ranking comes off PriceCharting's set listings; each candidate was then re-read from that card's own product page, which is a different page with different columns, and the two readings compared. 176 of 177 candidates agreed. The other one are not dropped whole: it is one FIGURE on each that could not be confirmed, so each is off the list ranked by that figure and still on the other one if its other column checked out. Charizard [Rainbow Foil] #6, 1999 Topps TV, PSA 10: the PSA 10 guide value moved between the two reads, so we hold two correct readings of it rather than one. A figure that was read once is not published here, because the column names on those two pages do not mean the same thing and a single read cannot tell.

Prices move every day and this page does not. The date above is the date the numbers were read, and if it looks old then the numbers are old.

Showing our work

How these lists were made

Where the numbers come from

PriceCharting's own price guide: pricecharting.com, methodology published at https://www.pricecharting.com/page/methodology. It is the same source the set guides, the Pokedex pages and the two site-wide value lists here are priced from, so a Topps card on this page and the same card on any other page of this site cannot disagree.

How we know these are the top 100 and not the first 100 we found

There is no all-of-Topps listing to sort and their one price sort works a bucket at a time, so the top of any one bucket says nothing about the top of the rest. Every Topps bucket was pulled and ranked here: 33 buckets, 2,701 products, of which 2,492 carry an ungraded value and 1,472 a PSA 10 value. Each hundred is the top of all of those, not the top of a sample.

Checked twice, on purpose

Every candidate for either list was read again from its own product page, a different template with different columns, and 176 of 177 agreed within 15%. That is the check that catches a column read off the wrong header, which on one card elsewhere on this site is a 21 times error that still looks like a reasonable price. Both ranking columns are gated on it independently, so a card whose raw price confirmed and whose PSA 10 price did not appears on one list and not the other.

What a row is

One PriceCharting product, which is one printing of one card. "Charizard #6", "Charizard [Sparkle] #6" and "Charizard [Tekno] #6" are three rows, not one, and the part in brackets is the finish. A guide value is computed across the sales that guide tracks rather than quoted from one of them, so treat a row as "what a loose copy of this printing is worth", not as a quote for a specific card.

Why the set names look wrong

They are PriceCharting's, and PriceCharting files these cards by card type rather than by the set Topps actually shipped. "2000 Topps TV Heroes & Villians" was never a Topps release; it is where two different sets' character cards ended up. The guide lists Topps' own 12 sets with their dates and card counts, and maps every one of these buckets onto the release it came out of.

What was taken out

Nothing. Every row flagged as possibly not a single card was checked by hand and every one of them turned out to be a card, so no row was removed from either ranking. Sealed Topps product is not ranked here at all: the crawl behind these figures excludes it, and unopened boxes have their own list at the 100 most expensive sealed Pokemon products.
