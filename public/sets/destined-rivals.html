Quick facts

When did Destined Rivals come out, and how big is it?

  • Released May 30, 2025, which was 17 months ago.
  • The set is 182 cards on paper, but there are 244 in total. The extra 62 are secret rares numbered past the printed count.
  • 62 of the 244 cards sit at a chase rarity, which is every tier tagged CHASE in the rarity breakdown below. That is what the checklist holds, not how often any of it turns up.
  • Only 6 Hyper Rares exist in the entire set.
  • The chase card is Team Rocket's Mewtwo ex (Special Illustration Rare), sitting around $289 raw, and $761 in a PSA 10.
  • We have 25 rips of this set on the channel.
  • 147 of the 244 cards belong to somebody by name. 83 of those are Team Rocket's, and the rest are Cynthia's, Ethan's, Arven's, Marnie's, Misty's, Steven's or an Emcee's.

That PSA 10 figure is graded sales data from pokemonpricetracker.com, read November 14, 2026. The raw figure beside it is the TCGplayer market price for an ungraded copy, which is a different measurement read on a different day.

What is actually rare

Destined Rivals secret rares and rarity breakdown

Destined Rivals has 62 cards numbered past the printed set, #183 to #244, against a printed total of 182. That is what collectors mean by a secret rare: the number runs past the total printed beside it. Each one's printed rarity is on the ladder below.

Prices are TCGplayer market and lowest-listing prices, read on November 14, 2026. They move every day, so treat them as a rough idea and not a quote. Product photos are TCGplayer's. We are not a shop and we do not sell any of this.

A Pokemon Center figure is that exact product's price in Pokemon's own shop, read by hand on August 17, 2026. Pokemon Center is the manufacturer selling its own product, so this site treats its price as the suggested retail price: what sealed Pokemon should cost. The multiple beside it is the market price divided by it, and its name (about retail to 1.15x, a shop's margin to 1.5x, scarcity pricing to 2x, double and up past 2x) is this site's rule of thumb, not a measurement. A shop figure is one listing of that product on one day at a retailer selling its own stock, dated beside it and linked to that retailer's page here. It is an example, not a score for the shop.

Cost per pack is the market price divided by the pack count printed on each card above, so you can check it. Sleeves, dice, decks and promo cards are counted as worth nothing, which flatters every box that includes them. Anything whose pack count is not in our data gets no per-pack figure rather than a guessed one, which is why blisters, tins and collection boxes have none, and why sets from before 2023 have none either: our counts are sourced for the current era and were not the same in earlier ones. What each product has held, and when it changed, is on how many packs are in it.
