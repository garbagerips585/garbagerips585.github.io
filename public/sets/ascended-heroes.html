Quick facts

When did Ascended Heroes come out, and how big is it?

  • Released January 30, 2026, which was 9 months ago.
  • The set is 217 cards on paper, but there are 295 in total. The extra 78 are secret rares numbered past the printed count.
  • 78 of the 295 cards sit at a chase rarity, which is every tier tagged CHASE in the rarity breakdown below. That is what the checklist holds, not how often any of it turns up.
  • Only 2 Mega Hyper Rares exist in the entire set.
  • The chase card is Mega Gengar ex (Special Illustration Rare), sitting around $668 raw, and $1,350 in a PSA 10.
  • We have 67 rips of this set on the channel.
  • At 295 cards this is the biggest English set with a guide on this site. The smallest, Celebrations, is 25.
  • 50 of those cards are Trainers rather than Pokemon. Trainers are the part of a set new collectors flick past, and they are what actually makes a deck run.

That PSA 10 figure is graded sales data from pricecharting.com, read November 10, 2026. The raw figure beside it is pricecharting.com's price guide value for an ungraded copy, which is a different measurement.

What is actually rare

Ascended Heroes secret rares and rarity breakdown

Ascended Heroes has 78 cards numbered past the printed set, #218 to #295, against a printed total of 217. That is what collectors mean by a secret rare: the number runs past the total printed beside it. Each one's printed rarity is on the ladder below.

A Pokemon Center figure is that exact product's price in Pokemon's own shop, read by hand on August 17, 2026. Pokemon Center is the manufacturer selling its own product, so this site treats its price as the suggested retail price: what sealed Pokemon should cost. The multiple beside it is the market price divided by it, and its name (about retail to 1.15x, a shop's margin to 1.5x, scarcity pricing to 2x, double and up past 2x) is this site's rule of thumb, not a measurement. A shop figure is one listing of that product on one day at a retailer selling its own stock, dated beside it and linked to that retailer's page here. It is an example, not a score for the shop.

Cost per pack is the market price divided by the pack count printed on each card above, so you can check it. Sleeves, dice, decks and promo cards are counted as worth nothing, which flatters every box that includes them. Anything whose pack count is not in our data gets no per-pack figure rather than a guessed one, which is why blisters, tins and collection boxes have none, and why sets from before 2023 have none either: our counts are sourced for the current era and were not the same in earlier ones.
