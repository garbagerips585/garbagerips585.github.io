What we have opened

Ordered by hits, which is the question asked. That order is a count, not a verdict: the kind with the most hits is usually the kind we opened most of, so every card carries the rate and the rips it is over, at the same size, right underneath. Most hits is single pack. The best RATE over 12 or more answered rips is collection box at 92.3%.

Rips are videos, packs are packs, and boxes are neither. An elite trainer box holds 9 packs and this channel films them one at a time, so 57 elite trainer box rips are nothing like 57 boxes. Packs is packs ripped on camera, out of the rips that state a count. Boxes is a floor worked out from the Pack number column: if pack 1 turns up in three videos of the same product and set then three boxes were opened, so that figure can only climb as more rips are logged. Across the kinds whose pack count is published, those boxes hold 154 packs between them and the rips of them count 116. Those are two different quantities and neither is the other: some of the difference is packs not opened on camera yet, and some is packs opened in one of the 13 rips that state no count.

The best cards, three ways

"Best" is not one thing. The first two land on the same card at the moment, which is not the usual case and will not stay true: they are two different measurements and the ungraded top and the graded top come apart as soon as a cheaper card with a strong graded price turns up. Ungraded figures are pricecharting.com's price guide value for an ungraded copy, read August 16, 2026. A guide value is computed across the sales PriceCharting tracks, which is a wider set of venues than any one marketplace. 2 cards of the 5,181 priced here are not listed by PriceCharting and keep their TCGplayer market price via TCGdex instead.

Best card, ungraded $175 Mega Greninja ex, Chaos Rising

The highest guide value among the 162 of 195 cards in the rip log that carry one. It came out of elite trainer box, at least 356 packs into the channel.

Best card, PSA 10 $906 Mega Greninja ex, Chaos Rising

A different question from the one beside it, and it rests on a much smaller set: 35 of the 195 cards in the rip log have a graded figure at all. Nothing here has been graded. This is what the guide says a 10 sells for.

Best return a rip at least $16.19 Collection Box

Guide value of every priced card out of it, over its 13 answered rips. At least is load bearing: a card with no guide value counts as nothing in that sum, so the figure is a floor and can only climb. Only kinds with 12 or more answered rips are eligible.

Does it matter which pack

The oldest superstition in the hobby, and this log can test it, because the rip sheet records which pack out of the box each video opened. The best position with a real sample behind it is pack 6 at 66.7% over 24 answered rips, against 49.1% across every answered rip.

Which sets have been kind

Hit rate is the share of answered rips from that set that produced something worth keeping. Sets with fewer than 12 answered rips do not get a number: at that size it would be noise dressed up as a fact. Rips is every rip of that set, and where some of them have no answer yet the cell says how many do, because the rate is only over those. Packs is packs ripped on camera, from the rip log's own set by set breakdown, so a rip that opened two sets charges each of them its own packs rather than both of them all of them.

Most rips produce nothing

148 of the 291 answered rips produced nothing worth keeping, which is 50.9% of them. That is the channel, not a bad patch: it is why it is called Garbage Rips. The drawing is every run of the same result in a row, so you can see how long the bad ones actually go.

What has actually come out

Counted from the Hit Card cell of the rip log, which is where the cards that came out are written down by hand, so this is a separate tally from the hit rates above and the two will not line up. A rip counts once per rarity however many cards of that rarity came out of it, so these are counts of rips rather than of cards: 143 tags across 131 rips against the 195 cards the log records, and the widest gap on one rip is 14 cards under 5 tags. That is on purpose: this band is about how often a rip produces each kind of card, and counting one opening five times would let a single lucky box outweigh a month of them. The cards themselves are listed one by one on the best pulls page. They are totals, not rates: a set that gets opened more will show more of everything. A fuller log makes these counts more complete, not more predictive: they say what came out of the packs we opened, never what will come out of yours.

WHY SOME CARDS CARRY NO FIGURE. 18 ARE FROM SETS WHOSE CHECKLIST THIS SITE DOES NOT HOLD. 10 NAME A CARD THAT IS NOT ON THE CHECKLIST FOR THE SET THEY NAME, WHICH IS A SPELLING TO FIX IN THE SHEET. 4 COULD BE MORE THAN ONE PRINTING OF THE SAME CARD, AND THIS PAGE WILL NOT PICK ONE TO WIN A NUMBER. A CARD WITH NO FIGURE IS STILL A HIT AND STILL COUNTS IN EVERY RATE ABOVE.

HOW THIS IS COUNTED. A rip counts once its outcome is KNOWN, and there are two ways the rip log says so: the Has Hit column is ticked yes or no, or the My Hits tab names the cards that came out of it, which is the same person answering the same question a different way. 1 rip on this page is here on the second route, and that route can only ever add a HIT, because nobody writes down the cards that did not come out. So while that number is above zero the rate leans very slightly high, by at most 1 in 291. A rip with neither answer is absent rather than assumed. "Hit" means a card worth keeping, judged by eye, not a fixed rarity threshold. These are observed results from one person opening packs on camera, not official pull rates: The Pokemon Company does not publish those, and anyone who tells you they know them is guessing. Small samples are labeled rather than rounded into confidence. Numbers move as more rips are logged.
