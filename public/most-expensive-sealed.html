The 100 most expensive sealed Pokemon products

Booster boxes, elite trainer boxes, single packs from 1999 and a lot of freight cases. Ranked by TCGplayer's own market price for their English Pokemon catalog, read on the date below.

Home / Most expensive sealed

What this list actually is

One marketplace, one number, one day

Every figure here is TCGplayer's own Market Price for that product, read on August 22, 2026. Market Price is their figure, worked out from recent completed sales on their marketplace. It is not the cheapest copy on sale right now, it is not what one sold for last week, and it is not an appraisal. The cheapest live listing is printed next to it, on the 97 rows that have one, so the two cannot be mistaken for each other. The other 3 say so instead of showing a figure: nothing is listed for sale, or the listings carry no asking price.

What this ranking cannot see. Japanese sealed product is not in it, because it is a separate catalog on the same site. Neither are the sealed cases that change hands at auction houses, which is where most of the really old unopened product actually sells. And a listed price is not a sold price: some of these have one listing and no recent sales at all, which the listing count on every row will tell you.

The cheapest copy is often pricier than the market price, and that is not a mistake. 56 of these 100 have a lowest listing above their market price today. Market Price looks backwards at what actually sold; a listing is what somebody is asking right now. On thin, old or barely traded things, and most of this list is all three, the asking prices sit well above the last sales, and sometimes there is nothing for sale at all.

Prices move every day and this page does not. The date above is the date the numbers were read, and if it looks old then the numbers are old.

Showing our work

How this list was made

A hundred prices is a hundred chances to publish something nobody can check. Here is exactly where each one came from and how we know the list is not just the first hundred rows that came back.

Where the numbers come from

TCGplayer's own search service, the one behind their website: https://mp-search-api.tcgplayer.com/v1/search/request?q=&isList=false. It needs no key. Their official developer API has been closed to new applicants for years, so this is the route, and it is the same feed the set guides on this site already use for buy links and box prices. The field is marketPrice, filtered to productLine pokemon (English) and productType Sealed Products.

How we know it is the top 100 and not the first 100 we found

The search will not page past its ten thousandth result, so there is no way to walk all 2,896 products and sort them. Instead we set a price floor of $500.00, pulled every one of the 324 products at or above it, and ranked those. Then we checked the answer: a fresh query asking how many products sit at or above $1,725.16, the price of the hundredth row, came back with 100. Nothing above the cut was missed, so this really is the top hundred of the 2,896.

Checked twice, on purpose

Every one of the 100 prices was read a second time from a different TCGplayer endpoint, the one their product page itself uses, and 100 of 100 matched to the cent. The ranking was also re-run through their own server-side sort as a second opinion, and it named 100 of 100 of the same products. Where two sources disagree about a row, the row says so instead of quietly picking one.

What a row is

A row is a TCGplayer product, which is not always one physical card. Base Set Charizard is a single product whose listings cover both the 1st Edition and the Unlimited printing, and the feed publishes one Market Price for it rather than one per printing. So treat a row as "this product on this marketplace", not as a valuation of a specific copy in a specific condition.

Loose cards rather than boxes are the 100 most valuable raw cards, and the same cards graded are the 100 highest PSA 10 values. Want the other half of the question? Is it worth grading? does the subtraction on raw against graded prices, pack prices by set covers what a pack costs today, and cost to complete a set prices the whole checklist.
