The 100 most valuable raw Pokemon cards

Every one of these is a loose card, out of a sleeve, ungraded. Ranked by TCGplayer's own market price for their English Pokemon catalogue, read on the date below and checked against a second TCGplayer source.

Home / Most valuable cards

What this list actually is

One marketplace, one number, one day

Every figure here is TCGplayer's own Market Price for that product, read on August 16, 2026. Market Price is their figure, worked out from recent completed sales on their marketplace. It is not the cheapest copy on sale right now, it is not what one sold for last week, and it is not an appraisal. The cheapest live listing is printed next to it, on the 83 rows that have one, so the two cannot be mistaken for each other. The other 17 say so instead of showing a figure: nothing is listed for sale, or the listings carry no asking price.

Where a price guide disagrees with this list. A price guide will tell you something different, and both figures are real. PriceCharting's guide, read August 16, 2026, puts the same ungraded Charizard at $988 against the $10,000 market price above. The same split runs through Blastoise ($1,300 against $223). Venusaur's Shadowless printing has a market price and no guide value at all, because the guide file is ranked by PSA 10 value and stops before it. Market Price is what recently sold on THIS marketplace; a guide value is computed across the sales that guide tracks, which is a wider and different set of venues. Neither is the other's correction. The guide figures for those Base Set printings are on the Base Set print run guide, which carries the same explanation from the other side.

What this ranking cannot see. Graded cards are not in it: a PSA 10 of any of these is a different object at a different price and this feed does not carry one. Japanese cards are not in it either, because they are a separate catalogue on the same site. Nor are the six and seven figure cards people usually mean by most valuable, because those sell at auction houses and private sales that TCGplayer never sees.

The cheapest copy is often dearer than the market price, and that is not a mistake. 26 of these 100 have a lowest listing above their market price today. Market Price looks backwards at what actually sold; a listing is what somebody is asking right now. On thin, old or barely traded things, and most of this list is all three, the asking prices sit well above the last sales, and sometimes there is nothing for sale at all.

Prices move every day and this page does not. The date above is the date the numbers were read, and if it looks old then the numbers are old.

Showing our working

How this list was made

A hundred prices is a hundred chances to publish something nobody can check. Here is exactly where each one came from and how we know the list is not just the first hundred rows that came back.

Where the numbers come from

TCGplayer's own search service, the one behind their website: https://mp-search-api.tcgplayer.com/v1/search/request?q=&isList=false. It needs no key. Their official developer API has been closed to new applicants for years, so this is the route, and it is the same feed the set guides on this site already use for buy links and box prices. The field is marketPrice, filtered to productLine pokemon (English) and productType Cards.

How we know it is the top 100 and not the first 100 we found

The search will not page past its ten thousandth result, so there is no way to walk all 29,652 products and sort them. Instead we set a price floor of $300.00, pulled every one of the 380 products at or above it, and ranked those. Then we checked the answer: a fresh query asking how many products sit at or above $698.84, the price of the hundredth row, came back with 100. Nothing above the cut was missed, so this really is the top hundred of the 29,652.

Checked twice, on purpose

Every one of the 100 prices was read a second time from a different TCGplayer endpoint, the one their product page itself uses, and 100 of 100 matched to the cent. The ranking was also re-run through their own server-side sort as a second opinion, and it named 100 of 100 of the same products. Where two sources disagree about a row, the row says so instead of quietly picking one.

What a row is

A row is a TCGplayer product, which is not always one physical card. Base Set Charizard is a single product whose listings cover both the 1st Edition and the Unlimited printing, and the feed publishes one Market Price for it rather than one per printing. So treat a row as "this product on this marketplace", not as a valuation of a specific copy in a specific condition.
