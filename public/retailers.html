Which stores sell Pokemon cards

10 shops we could confirm sell Pokemon cards, by reading it on that company's own website with our own eyes, on the date printed against each one. What they stock, which department they file the cards under, and every price we have actually read.

Eight more chains were checked and are not on this list. Three served a bot challenge instead of a shop, one served a search page that loaded and never filled in, three served a page with no Pokemon cards on it, and one answered with its own error. They are named at the bottom with the reason, because "we could not read their site" and "they do not sell them" are two different sentences and only the first one is ours to write.

If you cannot see any, ask anyway

If you cannot see any, ask anyway. A lot of shops now keep trading cards behind the counter, in a locked case or in the back, and plenty do not put them on the sales floor at all. An empty peg is not the same as no stock, and people walk out of shops that had the thing every day of the week.

The question to ask is specific: do you have any Pokemon cards behind the counter. A staff member who has been asked ten times that morning will answer it in three seconds, and 'do you sell Pokemon cards' gets you pointed at an empty shelf.

This site cannot tell you which chains lock their cards up, because no chain publishes that. What is published, and is on this page where we could read it, is the department each one files the cards under. Where the shelf actually is inside that department moves store to store.

Ask about the restock day while you are there. Nobody publishes one, and the person stocking the shelf knows it.

Sells them

Yes, in stores. Barnes & Noble's own collectible card page says your local B&N is the best place to find collectible cards including Pokemon, and tells you to find a store to shop the selection.

Best Buy files them under Trading Cards, with a separate Trading Card Accessories category. There is no aisle to give here: Best Buy does not publish one and the trading card fixture is a recent addition that is not in the same place in every shop.

A price on their site is not always their price

A price on target.com, walmart.com, bestbuy.com or amazon.com is not automatically that chain's price. All four are a shop and a marketplace at the same time: some listings are stock the chain owns, and some are independent sellers using the chain's website as a storefront and setting whatever price they like.

  • The chain's own stock generally sits at or near the suggested price. The listings that make you gasp are usually somebody reselling after the chain sold out, and the same product will be back near the suggested price on the next restock.
  • So the useful move on those four sites is to find who is selling before you look at the number. Target prints a 'Sold and shipped by' line on a partner listing, Walmart prints a seller name and a seller rating, and Best Buy's own search sidebar carries a 'Sold & shipped by: Best Buy' filter, which is the fastest way to see only the chain's own stock.
  • This matters to this page because it decides what we are allowed to publish. Every price in the table below carries the seller, and every reading where the seller could not be established was thrown away rather than printed against a company's name.

Where this page stops

This one is about shops with a door.
